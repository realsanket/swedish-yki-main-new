## 1. Summary, the core and the daily load rules

### The goal

The goal is level 3 (CEFR B1) on the **Swedish YKI intermediate test**, in a subtest pair that Migri accepts for Finnish citizenship. Reach it as fast as you honestly can, without learning all of Swedish.

### The method in 8 lines

1. **Measure first.** A timed Day 0 placement decides which phase you start in.
2. **Learn the exam's task types** and the language moves ("functions") they need, not the whole language.
3. **Memorise one small core for both speaking and writing** ("The core", below): six answer shapes, about 60 sentence frames, 12 reason pairs, 10 reactions and 8 rescue phrases. The 20 slot templates and the banks in section 2 are a library to look things up in.
4. **Learn a small grammar "engine"** that prevents level-2 errors: verb in second place, the place of *inte*, *att*-clauses, questions, modal verbs and past forms.
5. **Practise with the substitution ladder:** hear it first, then say a sentence, swap a detail, add a reason, add a solution, change it, then produce it from memory for a new prompt.
6. **Every day, do one timed speaking task** (recorded) and **one typed writing task**, each checked against a list of required points.
7. **Every day, spend 10 minutes** on Finland-Swedish listening or reading, so the other subtest pairs stay open.
8. **A teacher rates your work every week.** Gates use the teacher's rating plus things you can count. You book an exam only on evidence, never on hope.

### Daily load rules (these override any day entry)

1. At most 6 new frames and 8 new cards a day.
2. One grammar point a day, stated in one sentence.
3. Review stops at 8 minutes, finished or not.
4. Hear each new line before saying it.
5. One speaking task and one typed task. A book task replaces the day's own prompt.
6. A day is 60 minutes. If a block is added, another is removed.
7. A day is done when a changed version of the task works, not when a list is recited fast.
8. If the "done when" fails, repeat the day tomorrow with new details.
9. If a day entry holds more than rule 1 allows, split it into day a and day b. Day b uses the same tasks with new details. The calendar slips, and that is intended.

**The honest cost.** Rule 9 stretches the first phase. Days 1 and 2 were rewritten to fit the rules and Day 3 is split into 3a and 3b, but Days 4–14 still hold 10–15 new items on several days, so rule 9 will split some of them: Phase 1 may take about 18–21 study days. The 84 days become a count of units, not dates. Days 5–14 have not been rewritten; that is left until rule 9 proves too rough in use.

### What is decided and what is open

**Decided:**
- The core track is **speaking + writing**, because one sentence bank serves both.
- Listening and reading get a light daily minimum, so the pairs listening + writing and reading + speaking stay open.
- The day is 60–90 minutes, with rules for scaling it to 30 or 120.
- The core and the library, fresh mock prompts, and where the core lives: see the Decisions box at the top (16 November 2026).

**Open:**
- **The exam date and the pair.** You choose them at booking time, from mock evidence (section 4.7).
- **Combining partial-day certificates.** Migri and OPH's citizenship page both say it works; one OPH sentence still says "same test day". Confirm at booking time (section 8).
- **Your real starting level.** Day 0 measures it.
- **Your daily study time.**
- **Teacher availability.**

### An honest note on time

Going from nothing to B1 takes roughly **350 to 960 hours**. That range is a research estimate taken from two general sources (Cambridge English and the US State Department), not a measured Swedish YKI figure.

The 84-day core of this plan, at 60–90 minutes a day, gives about **84 to 126 hours**. You are not starting from nothing: you have had about 51 evening lessons, but your level is unknown. So the 84 days are a **core cycle**, not a promise. Since 16 November 2026 they are also a default order, not dates: you move on by the load rules above, so Phase 1 alone may take about 18–21 study days. If the booking gate is not met after Day 84, you repeat Phase 4 with new prompts (section 4.6).

Test-takers who passed describe months of work, regular speaking practice, feedback and listening practice. **Nobody supports "templates alone".**

### 2.2 The five-step opinion answer (priority 2)

**Why:** opinion tasks appear in speaking (Din åsikt) and writing (the letter to the editor, at least three reasons [Official sample]). One structure serves both.

| Step | What to say | Swedish frame |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Opinion | Your view, in the first sentence | *Jag tycker att …* / *Jag håller med.* / *Jag håller inte med.* |
| 2. Reason 1 + example | The strongest reason, then a concrete example | *För det första …* *Till exempel …* |
| 3. Reason 2 (and reason 3 in writing) | The next reason(s) | *För det andra …* *För det tredje …* |
| 4. Other view | One disadvantage or opposite opinion | *Å andra sidan …* / *En nackdel är att …* |
| 5. Conclusion | Repeat your view, linked to the reasons | *Men för mig är fördelarna större än nackdelarna. Därför tycker jag att …* |

**[Unreviewed Swedish: have a teacher check it]**

**The trap:**
- After *För det första*, *Till exempel*, *Å andra sidan* and *Därför*, the **verb comes next**, then the subject: *För det första **sparar man** tid.*
- Never write *För det första man sparar tid.*
- Step 5 must agree with step 1. If you weighed both sides, say so in step 1 (*Jag tycker att det är bäst att göra båda*).
- **Speaking:** use at least 2 reasons. **Writing:** use at least 3.

### 2.3 Templates per function: T1–T16, frames with slots (priority 3; reference library)

**Why:**
- Reusable openings, closings, connectors and skeletons with blank slots are the safe kind of memorising. Whole prepared answers are risky, because a memorised answer that misses the task's points scores low.
- No official rule on memorised answers exists [Unknown]. Evidence for slot templates is weak: one test-taker's account, plausible, nothing official.
- **Rule:** fill every slot with the prompt's own details, and delete any line the prompt does not ask for.

Square brackets [ ] are slots. T17–T20 are in `YKI-NEW-TEMPLATES-T17-T20.md`.

**den, det, de in the slots (T3, T4, T5, T12).** The lines use *den* for an *en*-word (*en cykel – den*). With an *ett*-word use *det* and an adjective with *-t*: *Det är tre år gammalt.* With a plural use *de* and *-a*: *De är tre år gamla.*

### 2.9 The minimum grammar that prevents level-2 errors (priority 9, but learned in the first two weeks)

**Why:** "Basic structures often wrong" is a level-2 sign [Official]. These ten points cover the basic structures that the tasks above use. Everything else can wait (section 3.7).

**[Unreviewed Swedish: have a teacher check it]**

**G1. Verb in second place (main clauses).** The finite verb is the second part of the sentence. If anything other than the subject comes first, the subject moves **after** the verb. That includes a time, a place, a connector such as *därför, sedan, till exempel, för det första, tyvärr, å andra sidan*, or a whole *när*-clause or *om*-clause.
- *Jag jobbar i Helsingfors.*
- *I dag jobbar jag hemma.*
- *För det första sparar man tid.*
- *När jag var liten bodde jag i Pune.*

*och, men, eller* and *så* (meaning "so") do **not** change the order: *Jag är sjuk, så jag stannar hemma.*

**G2. Where *inte* goes.** The same rule applies to *alltid, aldrig, också, gärna, redan, fortfarande*.
- **Main clause, subject first:** *inte* goes after the verb. *Jag kan inte komma.* / *Jag har inte tid.*
- **Main clause, something else first:** verb, then the pronoun subject, then *inte*. *I dag kan jag inte komma.* / *Tyvärr har vi inte tid.* / *Därför fungerar den inte.* (Avoid *I dag kan inte jag komma*: that order stresses *jag*, like "not me". Ask the teacher.)
- **Subordinate clause** (after *att, eftersom, om, när, fast, som*): *inte* goes **before** the verb. *… eftersom jag inte har tid.* / *Problemet är att den inte fungerar.* / *Jag hoppas att du inte är sjuk.*

**G3. Questions.**
- **Yes/no questions start with the verb:** *Har du tid i morgon?* / *Kan jag betala med kort?*
- **Question-word questions:** question word, then verb. *När öppnar ni?* / *Hur mycket kostar den?*
- **Inside a sentence (an indirect question), use normal order:** *Jag undrar när kursen börjar.* (Not *när börjar kursen*.) / *Jag undrar om ni har öppet på lördag.*

**G4. Modal verb + infinitive, with no *att*.** After *kan, vill, måste, ska, får, skulle, brukar*:
- *Jag vill boka en tid.*
- *Jag måste jobba i morgon.*
- *Kan du hjälpa mig?*
- *Jag skulle vilja byta den.*

**G5. *att*-clauses** after *tycker, tror, vet, hoppas, säger*. The order is *att* + subject + (*inte*) + verb, with no inversion inside the clause.
- *Jag tycker att det är en bra idé.*
- *Jag tror att hon kommer i morgon.*
- *Jag hoppas att du blir frisk snart.*
- *Jag vet att han inte har tid.*

**G6. Present, past and perfect of 30 common verbs.** The columns are infinitive – present – past – perfect.

**Learn them in layers (the core, section 1):** first the present of all 30; then the past of 12 (*var, köpte, ringde, kom, glömde, gick, hjälpte, tog, blev, fick, sa, bodde*: the past forms this plan's own Swedish uses most); the perfect only inside chunks such as *Jag har bott i Finland i fem år*, *Jag har redan betalat* and *Har ni fått mitt mejl?*. ## 3. How we will learn it

### 3.1 The daily lesson format

The **60-minute and 90-minute days** use the same blocks in the same order.

| Block | 60 min | 90 min | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Review | 0–8 | 0–12 | Cards that are due (3.3). Read the English cue, then **say** the Swedish aloud before you turn the card. |
| 2. New | 8–18 | 12–27 | Today's chunks or template plus one grammar point. Hear each new line first (3.2, rung 0), then run the substitution ladder (3.2) on 2–3 lines. |
| 3. Speaking | 18–33 | 27–52 | One timed task (two on a 90-minute day): prepare, speak, record, listen twice, then redo once (3.4). |
| 4. Writing | 33–48 | 52–72 | One typed task. Copy the required points as tick boxes first. Write within the practice time. Check in the fixed order (3.4), then rewrite 1–2 sentences. |
| 5. Listening or reading | 48–56 | 72–84 | The daily minimum (section 7). |
| 6. Log | 56–60 | 84–90 | Error log (3.6), and make error cards for at most 3 of today's errors (3.3). |

**Scaling:**

- **30 minutes:**
  - Review: 5 minutes.
  - Speaking: 10 minutes, one task, recorded.
  - Writing **or** listening/reading: 10 minutes, alternating days.
  - Log: 5 minutes.
  - Each calendar day then takes **two** real days, so the 84 days become about 168.
- **120 minutes:** do the 90-minute day, then add:
  - 15 minutes of extra speaking (a question-bank book task, or an appendix prompt: seen practice).
  - 15 minutes of extra listening or reading (a second clip or notice).
  - Do not add new chunks. Volume of practice matters more than volume of content.
- **Writing time limits:** the daily writing tasks (15–18 minutes) are deliberately a little shorter than the mock limits, to build speed. The phase exits and the booking gate use the mock limits in section 6.2.
- **Light days** (marked "Light" in the calendar):
  - 30–40 minutes.
  - Do your due cards (review still stops at 8 minutes, 3.3).
  - Re-record one task from 6 days earlier from memory, changing one detail.
  - Do one listening or reading item.

### 3.2 The substitution ladder

The ladder turns one sentence into a reusable tool. **Rule: never use a memorised phrase if it does not answer the question.**

**[Unreviewed Swedish: have a teacher check it]**

| Rung | What you do | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Hear it first | Hear the model from your teacher, a Swedish voice, or the book's QR audio before saying it. Repeat it once. | You hear: *Jag kan inte komma i dag.* |
| 1. Base | Say it from the English cue: "I can't come today." | *Jag kan inte komma i dag.* |
| 2. Swap | Change one detail (time, place, thing, person). | *Jag kan inte komma på fredag.* |
| 3. Reason | Add *eftersom* (watch *inte*!) or *därför*. | *Jag kan inte komma på fredag eftersom jag jobbar.* / *… eftersom jag inte har tid.* |
| 4. Solution | Add a question or an offer. | *Kan vi ses på lördag i stället?* |
| 5. Change | Put the time first (verb next!), or switch to formal. | *På fredag kan jag inte komma.* / *Tyvärr kan jag inte komma på mötet på fredag. Skulle vi kunna ses på lördag i stället?* |
| 6. Memory | Say it and type it without looking. Then use it to answer a **different** prompt. | Prompt: cancel a doctor's appointment. *Tyvärr måste jag avboka min tid på fredag, eftersom jag jobbar. Kan jag få en ny tid?* |

### 3.3 The spaced review schedule

**Cards.**
- Use paper cards, or one line per card in a plain text file.
- The front of each card is English or a cue. The back is Swedish.
- Write the next review date on every card.

**Schedule.** A card made on Day N comes back on:
- Day N+1
- Day N+3
- Day N+7
- Day N+14
- Day N+30

If you get a card wrong, it restarts at N+1.

**Daily limits** (since 16 November 2026; load rules 1 and 3): **at most 8 new cards a day**, error cards included, and **at most 3 error cards a day**. **Review stops at 8 minutes**, finished or not; the rest wait for tomorrow. At about 12 seconds a card, 8 minutes holds about 40 reviews, which is what 8 new cards a day produce once every card comes back 5 times. If reviews still pile up, make fewer new cards.

**Card types:**
1. **Chunk cards.** "I've already paid." → *Jag har redan betalat.*
2. **Frame cards.** One core frame per card (2.0), for example "bill complaint, the amount" → *Jag tycker att [___] euro är ett rimligt belopp.* A whole template is no longer one card.
3. **Reason and reaction cards.** "It saves time + example." → *Det sparar tid. Till exempel …* (you add a new example each time).
4. **Error cards.** Your wrong sentence → the correct form + the rule (G1–G10).
5. **Number cards.** "9:45" → *kvart i tio*. "80" → *åttio*.

**What is reviewed when:**

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Daily | The cards that are due. |
| Light days | Re-record one older task from memory with a new detail. |
| Every 4 weeks (before each mock) | Five random prompts, each from a different engine (seen practice): say the first two sentences of each, in about 10 minutes. Then the 10 core reactions and the 12 core reason pairs from the English cue. This replaces the old recital of every template. |
| The week before an exam | Only the cards and the core; nothing new (section 4.6). |

### 3.4 Recording and feedback rules

1. **Record every speaking attempt** with a voice recorder, and name the file with the day and the task.
2. **Listen twice.**
   - **Pass 1, points:** tick each required point you covered.
   - **Pass 2, clarity:** mark every place where a stranger would not understand you.
3. **Redo once at once,** fixing the weakest part. **Never redo more than twice.** After that, move on: repeating one answer many times only memorises that answer.
4. **Type every writing task** in a plain text editor with spelling help turned off. The digital test may be typed [Unknown].
5. **No dictionary or translation during a timed task.** Look words up afterwards.
6. **Check writing in this order:**
   1. Every required point is covered.
   2. The verb is in second place (G1).
   3. *inte* is in the right place (G2), and *att*-clauses and indirect questions use normal order (G3, G5).
   4. Verb forms (G6).
   5. *en/ett* (G7).
7. **Weekly teacher pack:** your 2 newest recordings and 2 newest texts. Send the newest, not the best.
8. **Self-scores never pass a gate.** Gates use things you can count (points, time, test scores) plus a rating from someone else (3.5).

### 3.5 What the teacher must check, and how often

| When | What the teacher checks |
|---|---|
| Phases 1–2, about 30–40 lines a week | **Every bank line** in this plan: templates, reasons, reactions, grammar examples and rescue moves. Is it correct? Natural? Suitable in Finland? |
| Weekly | The teacher pack, using the rating sheet below. |
| Every 4 weeks | The mock (section 6): every task. |
| At each phase exit | The exit tasks. |
| Before booking | The booking gate rating (4.7). |

**Rating sheet** (one per task, about 3 minutes):

- **a) Required points:** the teacher writes how many of the listed points were covered.
- **b) Overall rating** [Plan's own scale, not an official YKI scale]:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Clear, with few errors. |
| **B** | Understandable with some errors. |
| **C** | Understandable with effort; errors often get in the way. |
| **D** | Often unclear or fragmentary. |

  A or B counts as a pass.
- **c) Top 3 errors only,** with corrections.
- **d)** Any bank line that sounds unnatural.
- **e)** One thing to keep doing.

**If there is no teacher:**
- A Swedish-speaking adult (not you) can fill in a) and b). Items c) and d) need a teacher.
- With **no outside rater at all**:
  - The Phase 1 exit needs no rater anyway.
  - You may pass the Phase 2 and Phase 3 exits on their countable items alone. Mark the pass **provisional**.
  - You may **not** book. The booking gate (4.7) always needs an outside rating.

### 3.6 How to log and fix errors

**Keep an error log** as a plain text table, one line per error:

| Date | Task | What I said/wrote | Correct form | Type | Times seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The **types** are:
- G1 verb second
- G2 *inte*
- G3 question or indirect question
- G4 modal + infinitive
- G5 *att*-clause
- G6 verb form
- G7 *en/ett*
- word choice
- **missing point**
- **over time**

Missing points and going over time count as errors.

**Fix routine:**
1. **Log every error the same day, but make cards for at most 3 of them** (the ones that block meaning or keep coming back). The others stay in the log and feed the weekly top-3 drill.
2. **Every week, on day 1:** take the 3 most frequent error types from last week. For each, write 10 new sentences of that type (5 minutes each). The teacher checks them in the next pack.
3. **"Red" errors:** an error type seen 3 or more times in one week is red.
   - Do a 10-sentence drill on it every day until you have 2 clean days.
   - Ask the teacher for 5 more correct examples of it.
4. **Recurring missing points:** if a point type keeps going missing (for example contact details), add it to the top of that template's card in capital letters.

### 3.7 What NOT to do

- **Don't memorise whole answers.** Learn slot templates and fill them with the prompt's details.
- **Don't study what you can defer:** the passive, the subjunctive, the pluperfect, long relative clauses, idioms, rare words, a native-like accent, literary Swedish. These are low value for a pass.
- **Don't use fancy formal phrases.** They risk mistakes and earn nothing at level 3.
- **Don't restart an answer,** and don't switch to English, Hindi or Marathi.
- **Don't pad.** Stop when every point is covered.
- **Don't translate whole texts.** Don't freeze on one word.
- **Don't re-listen to one clip to memorise it.** Memorising one clip trains nothing. Use a new clip each day.
- **Don't use Finnish-language YKI preparation material.** It is a different language, and its formats are not confirmed to match.
- **Don't look for leaked or "remembered" exam questions.** Cheating means failure and a 6- or 12-month ban [Official].
- **Don't book before the booking gate is met.** Registration is binding [Official].
- **Don't trust your own score for a gate.**
- **Don't skip the daily listening and reading minimum.** It keeps the other pairs open, and the speaking test itself starts with listening to a recorded voice.

### 5.1 Eight days in full detail

The minute plans are for a 60-minute day. On a 90-minute day, add the extras listed at the end of each day.

#### Day 1: say who you are

*Simplified on 16 November 2026 to fit the load rules (section 1).*

**Goal:** understand four questions and answer each in one full sentence.

**New frames (5):**
1. *Jag heter [Alex].* (My name is …)
2. *Jag kommer från [Indien].* (I come from …)
3. *Jag bor i [Esbo].* (I live in …)
4. *Jag talar [engelska, hindi och marathi].* (I speak …)
5. *Jag lär mig svenska.* (I am learning Swedish.)

**Questions to understand (4):** *Vad heter du?* (What is your name?) *Var kommer du ifrån?* (Where are you from?) *Var bor du?* (Where do you live?) *Vilka språk talar du?* (Which languages do you speak?)

**Instruction words to recognise (2):** *berätta* (tell), *skriv* (write).

**The one grammar fact:** the verb has the same form for every person: *jag bor, du bor*.

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Listen.** Hear each frame three times (from your teacher, a Swedish voice or the book's QR audio), with the English in view. Repeat after the voice. |
| 10–20 | **Recall and swap.** Cover the Swedish. Say each frame from the English. Then swap the detail three times (another name, country, town, language). |
| 20–30 | **Questions.** Hear the four questions in mixed order. Answer each in a full sentence within 10 seconds. |
| 30–40 | **Speaking task.** Card: *Berätta om dig själv.* (Tell about yourself.) Underline the instruction word. Record five sentences, listen once, redo once. |
| 40–50 | **Typing.** Set up å, ä and ö. Type the five sentences from memory, compare with the model, fix, and type them once more. |
| 50–55 | **Change check.** Answer the four questions again as another person: Sara, from Sweden, lives in Åbo, speaks Swedish and Finnish. |
| 55–60 | **Cards:** 5 frames and 3 questions (8 cards). Start the error log. |

**Model:**
> *Hej! Jag heter Alex. Jag kommer från Indien. Jag bor i Esbo. Jag talar engelska, hindi och marathi. Jag lär mig svenska.*

**Done when:** you answer 4 of 4 questions without reading, and the typed text has no more than 2 spelling slips. If not, repeat Day 1 tomorrow with new details.

**Common errors:**

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Jag är bor i Esbo.* | *Jag bor i Esbo.* | No *är* in front of another verb |

**Moved out of Day 1, and where each item lands:**
- *Jag jobbar som …* and *Jag har bott …*: Day 2.
- Numbers 0–12: Day 2. Numbers 13–20: Day 3 (Day 3a in the curriculum).
- G1 (verb second) with *Nu bor jag …*, *På fritiden …* and *I dag jobbar jag hemma*: the start of Day 3, which already drills time-first sentences. By rule 9, Day 3 splits into 3a (G1) and 3b (*inte*).
- The two phone lines: Day 4, which already teaches the phone frame.
- The *eftersom* rung of the ladder: Day 8.
- The 60-second, 8-sentence talk: it stays as the Day 14 exit target (E5 in 4.2). By then you have all 8 facts.
- The book warm-up `p8-U`: optional reading.

**On a 90-minute day, also:** make a second recording with 2 new details, and do the 10-minute easy-news routine (section 7).

#### Day 8: giving reasons, refusing politely

| Minutes | What you do |

**The 8 chunks:**
1. *Jag kan inte komma eftersom jag jobbar.*
2. *Jag kan inte komma eftersom jag inte har tid.* (Subordinate clause: *inte* before the verb.)
3. *Jag jobbar på lördag. Därför kan jag inte komma.* (Verb second after *Därför*.)
4. *Jag är sjuk, så jag stannar hemma.* (*så* does not change the order.)
5. *Jag vill gärna komma, men jag har redan andra planer.*
6. *Tyvärr kan jag inte komma.*
7. *Kan vi ses en annan dag i stället?*
8. *Passar det på söndag?*

**Question word to recognise** (moved here from Day 2): *varför* (why). Its answer often starts with *eftersom* (because).

**Speaking prompts:**
1. *Din kollega bjuder dig på middag på fredag. Du kan inte. Tacka, säg varför och föreslå en annan dag.*
2. *Din granne frågar om du vill följa med till simhallen på lördag. Du kan inte.*
3. *Din vän vill träffas i kväll, men du är trött.*

**Model (prompt 1):**
> *Tack för inbjudan, vad snällt! Tyvärr kan jag inte komma på fredag, eftersom jag jobbar på kvällen. Kan vi ses på lördag i stället? Då har jag tid.*

**Writing prompt:** *Din kollega Sara har bjudit dig på middag på fredag. Du kan inte komma. Skriv ett kort svar. Tacka, säg att du inte kan komma, förklara varför och föreslå en annan dag.*

**Required points (6):**
1. Greeting.
2. Thanks.
3. Say you cannot come.
4. The reason.
5. A new day, as a question.
6. Closing.

**Model:**
> *Hej Sara! Tack för inbjudan till middagen. Tyvärr kan jag inte komma på fredag, eftersom jag jobbar till klockan tio. Jag skulle gärna vilja träffas en annan dag. Passar det på lördag eller söndag? Hälsningar, Alex*

**Common errors:**

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *eftersom jag har inte tid* | *eftersom jag inte har tid* | G2, subordinate clause |
| *Därför jag kan inte komma.* | *Därför kan jag inte komma.* | G1 |
| *Tyvärr jag kan inte.* | *Tyvärr kan jag inte.* | G1 |
| *Jag kan inte att komma.* | *Jag kan inte komma.* | G4 |

## 7. The listening and reading minimum

### 7.1 Why it matters

- **It keeps two pairs open:** listening + writing, and reading + speaking.
- **Speaking needs it too.** In the phone tasks you must understand a recorded voice that you cannot ask to repeat.
- **It can't be faked.** Listening and reading cannot be templated; they need real comprehension.
- **It gives cheap points.** Numbers, dates and the meaning-flip words are the cheapest points you can earn.

### 7.2 What to use

- **Yle's easy-Swedish news, "Nyheter på lätt svenska".** It is Finland-Swedish, with sound and text. Use a **new** item every listening day.
- **Real Finland-Swedish notices:**
  - the Swedish side of bilingual signs, leaflets, timetables and shop notices;
  - notices from a housing company;
  - letters from health services or the city.
- **Short texts the teacher hands out.**
- **Which accent the exam uses is unknown** [Unknown]. Practising with Finland-Swedish is this plan's choice, not an exam fact.

### 7.3 The 10-minute listening routine (4 days a week)

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | Read only the headline. Guess 3 words you expect to hear. |
| 1–4 | Listen **once** without the text. Write 3 facts: who, what, where. Do not stop at unknown words. |
| 4–6 | Listen again. Write every number, date, time, name and place. |
| 6–8 | Read the text. Check your facts and numbers. Underline 3 things you missed. |
| 8–10 | Read 3 sentences from the text aloud. Make 2 cards: one word, one number or date phrase. |

### 7.4 The 10-minute reading routine (2 days a week)

**[Unreviewed Swedish: have a teacher check it]**

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–1 | What kind of text is it (an ad, a notice, a letter, news), and what is it about? Take 20 seconds. |
| 1–4 | Answer the 5 notice questions: **what, when, where, for whom, cost or condition.** |
| 4–6 | Find any meaning-flip words (2.6). Does one change the meaning (*inte, stängt, inställt, i stället*)? |
| 6–8 | Write 3 true/false statements about the text, one of them false, and answer them the next day. |
| 8–10 | Make 2 cards. Note one pair of words with the same meaning (for example *billig / prisvärd*); questions often use a different word from the text. |

### 7.5 Exam habits to practise in every item

- **Read the question first.** Know which question word you need (*vem, vad, var, när, hur många, varför*).
- **Answer every item.** In the reading sample, wrong answers are not penalised [Official sample].
- **For an open answer,** write 1–4 words you heard or read.
- **Don't translate the whole text.** Find the keyword and read only the sentence around it.
- **If you're stuck, move on** and come back at the end.
- **Once a month,** work out your facts-right and numbers-right percentages, and enter them in the progress record.

## 8. Honest limits, open questions, and the pre-registration checklist

### 8.1 What this plan cannot do

1. **It cannot guarantee level 3.** Only official raters give grades. The teacher's A–D rating is not an official grade either.
2. **It may not be enough time.** From nothing, B1 takes roughly 350–960 hours (an estimate, not a YKI measurement). The 84-day core gives about 84–126 hours. Your starting level is unknown, so the core may need repeating.
3. **The templates will not cover every task.** In one practice book, about 14% of speaking tasks and 8 of 21 writing tasks fall outside the top five functions [Book count]. Twenty templates, grouped into six engines, reduce this gap; they do not remove it. Real tasks may differ again.
4. **Its Swedish is unreviewed.** The plan cannot check itself. A teacher must.
5. **It cannot copy the digital test.** The screens, typing rules, headsets and timers are unknown.
6. **The listening accent is unknown.** Read-aloud practice is weaker than real audio.
7. **Self-checks miss your own repeated errors.** That is why the gates need an outside rating.
8. **Writing may be the hard part.** One old study (2015) found writing to be the weakest subtest in that year's group. It is old and covers one group.
9. **Published results are not a pass rate.** One table of Swedish intermediate results (2020–2024) shows the share at level 3 or above falling from about 63% to 46%. The table does not define its terms, so it is **not** a pass rate. It is still a reason not to rely on templates alone.
10. **The certificate is not the whole test of your Swedish.** Migri also compares your real language use when you deal with them [Official]. A minimum certificate does not remove the need for usable Swedish.
11. **The citizenship rules are changing.** New rules start on 1 January 2027, and applications received from 1 March 2027 follow them. They add a separate citizenship (society) test. The language requirement appears to continue, but the law text was not checked.
