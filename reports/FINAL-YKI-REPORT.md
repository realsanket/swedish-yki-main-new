## 1. The one-page version

**What "pass" means.**
- The intermediate Swedish YKI gives a separate grade for each of four subtests: listening, speaking, reading, writing.
- Level 3 (CEFR B1) is the pass grade. Level 4 is higher. Below 3 means "not met" for that subtest only.
- There is no overall pass or fail. No subtest can make up for another.

**For citizenship you do not need level 3 in all four.** You need level 3 or 4 in one of these pairs:

| Pair | Why you might pick it |
|---|---|
| Speaking + writing | Both can be trained by practising and getting feedback |
| Listening + writing | Fits if you hear Swedish better than you speak it |
| Reading + speaking | Fits if writing is your weak spot |

Migri says two separate certificates can together make a pair. Both reports that checked this agree. One official page seems to say otherwise, so **ask Migri before you book a "partial day"** (section 8).

**The honest timeline.**
- Going from nothing to B1 takes roughly **350 to 960 hours** in total. That range is the report's own estimate from two sources, not a measured Swedish-YKI figure.
- 120 minutes every day for 24 weeks gives only about 336 hours.
- So the exam is a **decision to make at each deadline**, not a date to aim at blindly.
- If your class has already given you many hours, count them. I don't know your current level.

**Do these three things now.**
1. E-mail Migri and ask: "Can I use a partial-day certificate (writing + listening, or speaking + reading) for citizenship?"
2. Start timing yourself on the exam tasks in section 4, **on top of** your class and the app. Don't just add more class.
3. Count your study hours so far and compare them with the 350 to 960 range.

**Decision dates (all spring 2027, all digital).** Registration is binding and almost never refunded.

|---|---|---|---|
| 23 Jan 2027 | All four subtests | 7–14 Jan | 190 EUR |

## 2. How the exam works

All of this is from OPH (the Finnish National Agency for Education) and the University of Jyväskylä (JYU), unless I say otherwise.

**Running it**
- OPH runs the exam. JYU develops the tests and trains the raters. Local institutes host the sittings.
- Register online at yki.opintopolku.fi. Sign in with Suomi.fi or e-mail. Pay by Finnish online banking or card.
- The payment link is valid for 1 to 3 days. Registration counts only once paid.
- Since 1 Jan 2026, registration is binding and can't be moved to another date.
- Refund: only for your own illness. You need a medical certificate, you must cancel by 08:00 on the test morning, and you must apply within two weeks.

**Fees:** full intermediate test 190 EUR. Single subtests: listening 43, speaking 84, reading 43, writing 70. Booking all four singly (240 EUR) costs more than the full test. Review of a grade: 108 EUR per subtest.

**The day**
- About **5 hours including breaks**. Time per subtest is **not published**. Ignore anyone who quotes exact minutes.
- All Swedish tests in spring 2027 are digital, taken on a test-site computer. Details such as typing, headsets and timers were **not found**.
- Bring valid original photo ID: passport, ID card or residence permit card. A driving licence does not count. A photo of an ID does not count.
- Phones, watches and any smart or electronic device are collected, even during breaks. No dictionaries or study material. Pencil and eraser are provided.
- Latecomers can't sit the test. Cheating means failure and a 6 or 12 month ban.
- You may bring food for the breaks.
- **Every task and instruction is in Swedish only.**

**Helsinki-area centres.** The registration system shows which centre offers your date. The names listed are Arbis and Careeria (Helsinki), Axxell and Omnia (Espoo), and Axxell (Vantaa). Only Axxell Espoo is confirmed for the 14 Nov digital pilot.

**Results**
- About two months. A digital certificate for tests from 1 April 2026. No expiry.
- You can ask how the criteria were applied (free, within 14 days). You can ask for a re-rating within 30 days (108 EUR per subtest).
- Re-rating rarely changes anything: in 2025 there were 128 requests and 7 grades changed. Prepare to pass the first time.

**Nine topic areas** (stay the same at every level): me and my background; home and housing; shops and services; culture; travel; health and wellbeing; work; environment; society.

## 3. What level 3 means and what the raters tolerate

**In plain words:** you cope with ordinary practical situations and are understood, even with mistakes.

| Subtest | Level 3 in one line |
|---|---|
| Listening | You follow longer speech if the topic is fairly familiar. Unfamiliar topics or long stretches can still be hard. |
| Reading | You understand ordinary texts that need no special knowledge. |
| Speaking | You cope in everyday situations and take initiative. You are understood despite flaws. |
| Writing | You write simple, joined-up texts on usual topics. Some unclear bits are allowed. |

**Tolerated at level 3 (official criteria):**
- A clearly non-native accent, if you can be understood.
- Slow speech with some unnatural pauses.
- Words or structures carried over from your first language.
- Awkward grammar in demanding situations, if the message still comes across.
- Limited difference between formal and informal.
- Linking that is limited and repetitive, as long as your ideas join into a whole.

**What blocks level 3:** only routine one-line exchanges, frequent hesitation, leaning on the other speaker, basic structures often wrong, fragmentary writing. **A blank or unfinished task is the only error the sources treat as fatal.**

**Not published anywhere:** pass marks, marks per item, word counts for writing, how long you must speak, any rule about memorised answers, and which accent is expected. If a source gives you a number for these, it made it up.

**Practical meaning.** For a pass, correct basic sentences, covering every point in the task, and a few joining words beat fancy grammar. The sample answers in `claude.md` Part 2.4 (line 155) show the gap between "inadequate", "borderline 3" and "stronger 3". The borderline and stronger versions use almost the same simple Swedish. The stronger one just covers everything and links ideas.

## 4. The four subtests: what is known and what to do

The sample tasks are paper-era JYU practice items, not official test tasks. They show the **task types**. Your sitting may differ.

### Speaking (recorded; no live partner)

**Sample tasks:**
1. Prepare 1 minute (paper notes allowed). Talk 1.5 minutes about a person important to you.
2. Phone call: a buyer answers your "for sale" ad. You answer short turns of about 10 to 25 seconds.
3. Phone call: you ring a repair company about something still broken. Six turns of 10 to 25 seconds.
4. A 20-second voicemail: you will be late.

The other voice is a recording. You can't ask it to repeat.

**What to do**
- Learn a fixed first sentence for each task type, so you never freeze. *Hej, det här är [namn].*
- Answer the question in the first sentence. Add one reason (*eftersom*) and one example (*till exempel*).
- Use short sentences, five to eight words.
- If you slip, say *Förlåt, jag menar...* once and carry on. Don't restart.
- Stop when every point is covered. Padding costs time.
- Never switch to English, Hindi or Marathi.
- Practise with a timer and **record yourself every day**. Listen once for "did I answer every point?" and once for "was I understandable?"
- When you know the English but not the Swedish, say a simpler true thing, or describe the word: *Det är en sak som man använder för att...*
- Practise with some background noise. A 2022 test-taker said the room was noisy. If noise worries you, special arrangements must be requested **at registration**.

### Writing (handwritten in the paper era; typing in 2027 not confirmed)

**Sample tasks:**
1. Informal e-mail to a friend about an event: what, when, where, plus two more details. Open and close properly.
2. Letter to the editor: your opinion with **at least three reasons**.
3. Formal complaint about a bill: which bill, **at least two reasons**, a suitable amount, contact details.

**What to do**
- Before you write, copy the task's required points into a checklist. Tick each one as you write it.
- Match the register: friend or company. Greeting and closing matter.
- Number your reasons: *För det första... För det andra... För det tredje...*
- Aim for about 8 to 12 short sentences per task. This is coaching advice. **No official word count exists.**
- Keep it plain. Fancy formal phrases risk mistakes and earn nothing at level 3.
- Check in this order: every point covered, then verb in second place, then *inte*, then *en/ett*.
- Get feedback from a teacher. Self-checking misses your own repeat errors.
- **Practise typing on a keyboard too.** The 2027 test is digital.

### Listening

**Official facts:** three task types: multiple choice, true/false, open questions (short written answers). One report says the sample recordings play twice, but another could not read the items to confirm it, so **play counts are unverified**. The sample has three recordings.

**What to do**
- Read the question first. Know which question word you need: *vem, vad, var, när, hur många, varför*.
- Numbers, dates, times, prices and places are the cheapest points. Drill them.
- Listen hard for words that flip the meaning: *inte, aldrig, ingen, men, i stället, tyvärr, inställt*.
- Don't freeze on one word. Guess from what comes before and after. Never leave an item blank.
- Open answers: one to four words you heard. Rough spelling is fine if the meaning is clear.
- **Real audio to use:** Yle "Nyheter på lätt svenska" (Finland-Swedish, with text). Listen once and write three facts. Listen again and write the numbers. Read the transcript. Try a new clip the next day. Memorising one clip trains nothing.
- Reports disagree on whether the exam audio is Finland-Swedish. Practise with Finland-Swedish and don't assume it is easy.

### Reading

**Official facts:** multiple choice, true/false, open questions. Wrong answers are not penalised in the sample, so answer everything. Sample texts: an eye-drop advert, a feature article full of figures, a news report about a police chase.

**What to do**
- Read the questions first. Find the keyword in the text. Read only the sentence around it.
- Expect different words with the same meaning (*billig / prisvärd*). Don't hunt for exact matches.
- Guess from context and from English look-alikes (*information, problem, kontakt, service*).
- Don't translate the whole text.
- Practise number-heavy texts: find the right number and what it counts.
- If you're stuck, move on. Come back at the end.

### Instruction words come first

Because every instruction is in Swedish, learn these before any sample task: *läs, lyssna, välj, skriv, svara, berätta, förklara, beskriv, jämför, föreslå, motivera, rätt/fel, åtminstone*.

## 5. What to learn first, and what to skip

### Learn first, in this order
1. The instruction words above.
2. Numbers, times, dates, prices. Careful: **halv tre = 2:30**. *Kvart över nio* = 9:15.
3. About 100 core words, then about 200 more by theme. Lists are in `claude.md` Appendix F (line 2011).
4. About 20 to 50 common verbs in the present tense, then their past forms.
5. Word order: the verb comes second. *I morgon har jag tid.*
6. *inte* after the verb. Questions. Modal verbs: *kan, vill, måste, ska* + infinitive.
7. Joining words: *och, men, eftersom, så, att*.
8. Phone openings and closings. Letter openings and closings.
9. Past tense, and *har* + past form.
10. Vocabulary for the top topics (section 6).

### Defer (low value for a pass)
Passive, subjunctive, pluperfect, long relative clauses, idioms, rare vocabulary, native-like accent, literary Swedish.

### Memorise patterns, not whole answers
No official page says memorised answers are allowed or banned. The criteria reward answering **this** task. A memorised essay that misses the points scores low. So:
- **Safe:** openings, closings, joining words, request and complaint skeletons with blank slots.
- **Risky:** whole prepared answers.
- **Impossible to memorise:** understanding new audio, reading new texts, reacting to the recorded voice, and timing.

### Where the marks per hour are (all expert estimates, no measured figures)

| Do first | Do later | Skip for now |
|---|---|---|
| Basic word order and the common verbs | Easy-news listening (Yle) | Advanced grammar |
| Numbers, times, dates, prices | Writing feedback from a tutor | Entertainment with no exercises |
| Reusable sentence patterns with blanks | Language café or tandem partner | Reading a whole textbook cover to cover |
| Recording and reviewing your speaking | A full mock test (week 14 or later) | Memorising full model answers |
| Short notices and ads for reading | | Finnish-language YKI packs |

**A complete complaint model** (from `claude.md`, template W3):

För det andra har jag redan betalat för reservdelen. Jag tycker att 320 euro är rimligt. Jag väntar på ert svar. Ni når mig på 040 123 4567. Med vänliga hälsningar, Rahul Sharma

It covers the bill, two reasons, an amount and contact details. That is the checklist the sample complaint asks for.

**An informal message model** (template W2):

> Hej Johan! Jag vill berätta om en filmfestival. Den börjar på fredag klockan 18 i Kulturhuset. Biljetterna kostar bara 10 euro. Jag vet att du gillar komedier, så jag tänkte att vi kan gå tillsammans. Vill du följa med? Kram, Rahul

### Grammar slips that cost marks (from `claude.md`)
- *Imorgon jag har tid* — wrong. The verb must be second.
- *Jag vet att han kommer inte* — wrong. In a "that" clause, *inte* goes before the verb.
- *Jag kan att hjälpa* — wrong. No *att* after *kan*.
- *mer billig* — use *billigare*.
- Mixing up *halv* and *över* in times.
- Padding with memorised sentences that don't answer the task.

## 7. How long it really takes

**Two anchors, both inferences, neither Swedish-YKI-specific:**
- Cambridge English: about 350 to 400 guided hours from beginner to B1.
- US State Department: Swedish is a "close to English" language. Their figure is 24 weeks of full-time study (552 class hours plus self-study), which comes to roughly 960 hours. Their target level is not B1 and is probably higher.

**Hours you can actually get** (weeks × 7 days × minutes ÷ 60):

| Weeks | 30 min/day | 60 min/day | 90 min/day | 120 min/day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 28 h | 56 h | 84 h | 112 h |
| 12 | 42 h | 84 h | 126 h | 168 h |
| 16 | 56 h | 112 h | 168 h | 224 h |
| 24 | 84 h | 168 h | 252 h | 336 h |

**No cell reaches 350.** You need about 25 weeks at 120 minutes a day, 33 weeks at 90, or 50 weeks at 60.

**Test-takers:** one blogger took 9 to 12 months to pass all four at level 3. Others took 6 to 10 months. All are anecdotes, and none says what the starting level was.

**Verdict (follows `claude.md`):**
- **23 Jan and 3 Mar 2027:** not realistic from A0 on the evidence. 3 Mar is at best a trial run.
- **17 Apr and 19 May 2027:** possible only with about 90 to 120 minutes a day, every day, with feedback, aimed at **one pair**. Even then it is not assured. No probability exists in the evidence.
- **Most realistic:** a first sitting after spring 2027. Reach about A2 through your class, then do 150+ hours of exam-style practice on one pair.
- Level 3 is communicate-and-be-understood, not perfect Swedish. That is why the trade is worth making.

**You are not starting cold.** You have a weekly class, the app, and the Gimara book. The book is the one `chatgpt.md` says candidates keep mentioning (anecdotal). **Use this time as a stack, not a replacement.** Class and app build the base. Daily timed exam tasks (section 4) are the new part.

The app's own YKI content (Lectures 23–52) was written by AI and, per the project handbook, has not been reviewed by a native speaker. Treat its Swedish like the phrase sheet above and show it to your teacher.

**Suggested weekly rhythm (60 to 90 min/day)**
- Mon, Wed, Fri: new patterns and production (speak or write).
- Tue, Thu: listening and reading with questions.
- Sat: record one speaking task and write one writing task. Review them.
- Sun: light review.
- Every two weeks: a self-check. Every four weeks: a longer timed run.

**Self-check every two weeks (your own benchmarks, not official):**
- Can I understand ten ordinary questions without English?
- Can I speak 45 to 60 seconds on an unseen everyday topic?
- Can I write a complete short message with every point?
- Can I get the main idea and key facts from an easy clip?
- Can I read a practical notice without translating each word?

**Register only when, on two mocks of your target pair in a row:**
1. Every required point is covered in each speaking and writing task.
2. You finish inside the time.
3. You get about 70% or more on listening and reading you haven't seen. This 70% is the report's own benchmark. No official threshold exists.
4. Someone outside the AI, such as your teacher, says your speech and writing are "understandable with some errors".

**Last week before the exam:** no new grammar. One timed task of each type per day, then rest. The evening before: pack ID, food and water, and plan travel with a buffer. Sleep. Never look for "leaked" or "remembered" exam questions. The ban is 6 to 12 months.
