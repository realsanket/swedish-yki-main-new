*This file goes with `YKI-EXAM-FIRST-PLAN.md` (section 2.3, templates T1–T16) and `YKI-EXAM-FIRST-CURRICULUM.md`. It adds four slot templates for the tasks in the practice book that no template T1–T16 covers, and it proposes where to teach them.*

**Source of the gap.** The learner's practice book, *Förbered dig för allmän språkexamen* (Gimara Oy), was catalogued task by task: 232 entries (225 tasks + 7 warm-up sections). **49 tasks fit no template T1–T16 well** [Book count]. Book tasks are named here only by their catalogue id, and the id starts with the book page: `p112-R2` is Reagera item 2 on page 112. No sentence from the book is copied. All examples are new situations.

**Since 16 November 2026, T17–T20 belong to the reference library.** The learner memorises only the core (plan section 1); the lines in this file are looked up when a task needs them. The T-numbers stay as aliases, so the book map still works.

**Second review (16 November 2026).** Each full example now has a **short version** next to it: learn the short one first, and add the expanded one later. A short model for a 20–25 second turn has about 40–60 words at most. **T20 is optional library material**, not a compulsory early block (T17 stays, because 18 book tasks need it). Success is marked **Green, Amber or Red** (plan section 1): Green, move on; Amber, move on and put the top 1–2 mistakes on the error list; Red, repeat the skill tomorrow with new details. Times and counts below are targets, never gates.

## 1. The templates

### T17. Polite objection: ask someone to stop, or stand up for yourself

**When to use:**
- Someone near you does something that bothers you or is unfair: noise, music, a phone call, pushing in, sitting in your seat, treating an animal or a child badly.
- A friend or colleague has let you down, and you want to say so calmly.
- Someone doubts you or complains about you, and you must stand up for yourself (your turn, your seat, your age, what really happened).
- It is face to face, or a phone call about your own behaviour. A shop, a firm or a bill uses T4 or T5 instead.

**The formula:** notice → ask → reason → thank (or say the next step).

**Step 1: notice.** Get attention softly, then name what you see.
- *Ursäkta, …* (Excuse me, …)
- *Förlåt att jag stör, men …* (Sorry to disturb you, but …)
- To a friend: *Du, [Sara], kan vi prata en sekund?* (Hey, Sara, can we talk for a second?)
- *Jag ser att [du pratar i telefon].* (I can see that you are talking on the phone.)
- *Jag tror att [du sitter på min plats].* (I think you are sitting in my seat.)

**Step 2: ask.** Say exactly what you want.
- *Skulle du kunna [prata lite tystare]?* (Could you talk a bit more quietly?)
- *Kan du vara snäll och [sänka musiken]?* (Could you please turn the music down?)
- When it is serious: *Det är inte okej att [lämna hunden i en varm bil].* (It is not OK to leave the dog in a hot car.)
- *Snälla, sluta [___].* (Please stop ___.)

**Step 3: reason.**
- *…, eftersom [jag försöker läsa].* (…, because I'm trying to read.)
- *Jag mår inte så bra, och [musiken är ganska hög].* (I don't feel very well, and the music is quite loud.)
- *Det kan vara farligt för [barnen].* (It can be dangerous for the children.)

**Step 4: thank, or say the next step.**
- *Tack för att du förstår.* (Thanks for understanding. Use it when you end your request, before the person has agreed.)
- *Tack, det var snällt av dig.* (Thanks, that's kind of you. Only after the person has agreed or helped.)
- If the person says no: *Då måste jag tyvärr prata med [personalen].* (Then I'm afraid I have to talk to the staff.)

**Part 2 lines: stand up for yourself, or talk to someone you know.**
- Your turn: *Ursäkta, men jag var här före [honom].* (Excuse me, but I was here before him.)
- Your seat: *Jag har en platsbiljett: vagn [fyra], plats [tolv].* (I have a seat reservation: car 4, seat 12.)
- Your age: *Jag förstår att du måste kontrollera, men jag är [tjugofem] år. Här är mitt id-kort.* (I understand that you have to check, but I am 25. Here is my ID card.)
- Someone complains about you: *Jag förstår att [ni är irriterade], men det stämmer inte riktigt.* (I understand that you are annoyed, but that's not quite right.) *Vi [stängde av musiken klockan elva].* (We turned the music off at eleven.) *Jag ber om ursäkt för [ljudet], och det händer inte igen.* (I apologise for the noise, and it won't happen again.)
- A friend or colleague: *Jag blev ledsen när [du pratade om mig med andra].* (I was hurt when you talked about me with other people.) *Vi bestämde att [___], men [___].* (We agreed that ___, but ___.) *Nästa gång vill jag att du [säger till först].* (Next time I want you to tell me first.) *Kan vi prata om det?* (Can we talk about it?)

**Full example** (Reagera, 20 s prep + 25 s [Teacher, unofficial]). Situation: you booked the laundry room in your building, and a neighbour has taken your wet washing out of the machine.

**Short (learn this first, 18 words):** *Ursäkta! Jag har bokat tvättstugan till klockan nio. Kan du vänta lite? Jag behöver tvätta i dag. Tack!*

(Excuse me! I've booked the laundry room until nine. Can you wait a bit? I need to do my washing today. Thanks!)

**Expanded (add later):** *Ursäkta! Jag ser att du har tagit ut min tvätt ur maskinen. Jag har bokat tvättstugan till klockan nio i kväll. Skulle du kunna vänta tills min tid är slut? Jag måste tvätta i dag, eftersom jag reser i morgon. Tack för att du förstår!*

(Excuse me! I can see that you've taken my washing out of the machine. I've booked the laundry room until nine tonight. Could you wait until my time is over? I have to do my washing today, because I'm travelling tomorrow. Thanks for understanding!)

The expanded version has about 45 words, which is about 25 seconds at a calm pace; the short one leaves you time to breathe.

**Common errors:**
- *Ursäkta, kan du att prata tystare?* → *Ursäkta, kan du prata lite tystare?* (G4: no *att* after a modal verb)
- *…, eftersom jag mår inte bra.* → *…, eftersom jag inte mår bra.* (G2: after *eftersom*, *inte* goes before the verb)

**Required points a rater would look for** [Plan's own]:
- **Stranger (part 1):** a polite opener; what the problem is; a clear request; a reason; a thank-you or a next step.
- **Standing up for yourself (part 2):** a polite opener; the fact (I was first, my seat, my age, what really happened); proof if you have it (a ticket, an ID card); a calm request; a close.
- **Friend or colleague:** what happened; how you feel; what you want next time; a way forward (*Kan vi prata om det?*).
- **Tone:** firm but polite. A bare *Sluta!*, or an insult, does not answer the task. Aim for about 25 seconds (a target, not a gate).

**Book tasks it covers (18):**
- Part 1, a stranger (Day 22 block): p34-R3 (boys teasing a classmate), p51-R10 (noisy children at the next table), p51-R12 (dog left in a hot car), p62-R4 (dumping an appliance in nature), p62-R8 (mistreating an animal), p72-R4 (a driver nearly hits you), p112-R2 (loud phone call in a library), p112-R11 (loud music on the bus), p112-R13 (noisy eater at the cinema), p113-R15 (a stranger's child and an unsuitable programme).
- Part 2, stand up for yourself or talk to someone you know (Day 24 block): p112-R3 (served after a later customer), p112-R4 (a doorman doubts your age), p112-R9 (someone in your reserved seat), p34-R14 (a friend spoke badly about you), p51-R2 (a friend returned your car with an empty tank), p87-R9 (a colleague broke an agreement), p112-R10 (a friend stays on their phone), p32-D11 (you are the one complained about: a 7-turn call).
- **Forced fits, said honestly:**

  - p113-R15 is a child, so use only the gentle lines (*Förlåt att jag stör …*, a simple reason) and no *Det är inte okej*.
  - p34-R3, p51-R12 and p62-R8 are about someone else's safety. Add an offer: *Ska jag ringa efter hjälp?* (Shall I call for help?)
- **Also helps** (catalogued under another template): p54-S2 (written: ask someone to stop sending gifts, T8) and p62-R2 (ask a relative to stop feeding ducks, T11).

### T18. Describe a habit, routine, hobby, place or thing

**When to use:**
- A Berätta topic (1 min prep + 1.5 min [Official sample timing]) or a Reagera item about **how you live**: your normal day, your habits, a hobby, how you get around, how you look after your health. This is part 1.
- A topic about **what something is like**: your home, your area, nature, a summer cottage, a book or film, your own style. This is part 2.
- T14 is for a person and T15 is for one past event. T18 is for what you **usually** do, and for what a place or thing **is like**.

**Part 1: habits, routines and hobbies** (Day 34)
- Open: *Jag vill berätta om [min vardag / mina vanor / min hobby].* (I want to tell you about my everyday life / my habits / my hobby.)
- How often:
  - *Jag brukar [gå upp klockan sju].* (I usually get up at seven.)
  - *Varje dag [cyklar jag till jobbet].* (Every day I cycle to work.)
  - *Ibland [jobbar jag hemma].* (Sometimes I work at home.)
  - *En gång i veckan [spelar jag badminton].* (Once a week I play badminton.)
  - *Jag [tränar] nästan aldrig.* (I almost never exercise.)
- Weekly habits use the plural day: *på lördagar* means every Saturday, while *på lördag* means this Saturday (G9).
- Order: *Först [äter jag frukost]. Sedan [tar jag bussen]. Efter jobbet [handlar jag].* (First I have breakfast. Then I take the bus. After work I do the shopping.)
- Hobby:
  - *Jag har [spelat schack] i [fem] år.* (I have played chess for five years.)
  - *Det kostar [inte så mycket]. Man behöver bara [ett bräde].* (It doesn't cost much. You only need a board.)
- Feeling:
  - *Det bästa är att [jag träffar mina vänner].* (The best thing is that I meet my friends.)
  - *Jag tycker inte om [att stressa på morgonen].* (I don't like rushing in the morning.)
- Then and now: *Förr [åt jag mycket godis], men nu [äter jag mer frukt].* (I used to eat a lot of sweets, but now I eat more fruit.)

**Part 2: a place or a thing** (Day 38)
- Open: *Jag vill berätta om [vår stuga / mitt kvarter / en bok som är viktig för mig].* (I want to tell you about our cottage / my neighbourhood / a book that matters to me.)
- Where:
  - *Den ligger [i Sibbo], ungefär [en timme] från [Helsingfors].* (It is in Sibbo, about an hour from Helsinki.)
  - *Det ligger nära [havet], bredvid [en liten skog].* (It is near the sea, next to a small forest.)
- What it is like:
  - *Den är [liten och gammal], men [mysig].* (It is small and old, but cosy.)
  - *Det finns [en bastu och en brygga].* (There is a sauna and a jetty.)
- What you do there:
  - *Där brukar vi [bada och grilla].* (There we usually swim and have a barbecue.)
  - *På vintern kan man [åka skidor].* (In winter you can ski.)
- Why it matters: *Den är viktig för mig eftersom [jag kan vila där].* (It is important to me because I can rest there.)
- For a message or a neighbourhood: *En fördel är att [det är lugnt]. En nackdel är att [bussen går sällan].* (One advantage is that it is quiet. One disadvantage is that the bus runs rarely.)
- Close (both parts): *Det var lite om [min hobby].* (That was a little about my hobby.) / *Därför tycker jag mycket om [___].* (That is why I really like ___.)

**Full example** (Berätta, 1 min prep + 1.5 min). Topic: a place you go to often.

**Short (learn this first, 48 words):** *Jag vill berätta om simhallen nära mitt hem i Esbo. Den är ny och ljus. Det finns två bassänger och en bastu. Jag brukar simma där två gånger i veckan. Först simmar jag, sedan går jag i bastun. Den är viktig för mig eftersom jag kan vila där.*

(I want to tell you about the swimming hall near my home in Espoo. It is new and bright. There are two pools and a sauna. I usually swim there twice a week. First I swim, then I go to the sauna. It is important to me because I can rest there.)

**Expanded (add later):** *Jag vill berätta om simhallen i Hagalund, nära min lägenhet i Esbo. Den ligger bara tio minuter från mitt hem, bredvid en stor park. Simhallen är ganska ny och ljus, och det finns två bassänger och en bastu. Jag brukar simma där två gånger i veckan, på tisdagar och torsdagar. Först simmar jag en halvtimme. Sedan går jag i bastun och vilar lite. Ibland tar jag med min dotter, och då leker vi i den lilla bassängen. Det kostar inte så mycket, ungefär sex euro. Förr tränade jag nästan aldrig, men nu mår jag mycket bättre. Simhallen är viktig för mig eftersom jag kan glömma jobbet där. En nackdel är att det är mycket folk på lördagar. Det var lite om mitt favoritställe.*

(I want to tell you about the swimming hall in Tapiola, near my flat in Espoo. It is only ten minutes from my home, next to a big park. The swimming hall is quite new and bright, and there are two pools and a sauna. I usually swim there twice a week, on Tuesdays and Thursdays. First I swim for half an hour. Then I go to the sauna and rest a little. Sometimes I take my daughter, and then we play in the small pool. It doesn't cost much, about six euros. I used to exercise almost never, but now I feel much better. The swimming hall is important to me because I can forget work there. One disadvantage is that it is very crowded on Saturdays. That was a little about my favourite place.)

The expanded version has about 120 words. That fills 1.5 minutes at a beginner's pace and mixes both parts: a place plus a habit. The short one is about half; add one detail at a time until the talk fills the time.

**Common errors:**
- *Varje dag jag cyklar till jobbet.* → *Varje dag cyklar jag till jobbet.* (G1: after a time or frequency word, the verb comes next)
- *Jag brukar att simma två gånger i veckan.* → *Jag brukar simma två gånger i veckan.* (G4: no *att* after *brukar*)

**Required points a rater would look for** [Plan's own]:
- **Part 1, habits:** what you talk about; "how often" words (aim for two); the order of a normal day or visit (*först, sedan*); one feeling or reason; one change (then and now) or one advantage or disadvantage; a close.
- **Part 2, place or thing:** what it is and where it is; two details of what it is like; what you do there (*brukar*); why it matters (*eftersom*); one advantage or disadvantage; a close.
- **In a message** (p64-S2): a greeting, where you live, two details, one advantage, one disadvantage, a tip or an offer, and a closing.
- **Timing (a target, not a gate):** aim to keep the Berätta talk going for the full 1.5 minutes without repeating yourself. Plan one point for each 15 seconds.

**Book tasks it covers (21):**
- Part 1, habits, routines and hobbies (Day 34 block): p35-BB (your arguing habits), p52-B1 (you and time), p52-B2 (what kind of arguer you are), p52-B3 (your normal weekday), p53-B5 (how you get around), p73-BB (looking after your body), p73-BD (looking after your brain), p73-BE (staying healthy), p88-BD (a normal working day), p114-BF (how you spend money when you travel), p114-BI (your hobby), p106-D6 (a phone survey about your internet habits).
- Part 2, a place or a thing (Day 38 block): p12-D1 (a friend visits your flat: describe the home), p35-BC (your clothes and style), p62-R6 (what matters to you in a neighbourhood), p62-R7 (describe Finnish nature to a friend abroad), p62-BA (nature and your home surroundings), p63-BC (a landscape that matters to you), p64-S2 (a written message: describe your area to a friend who may move), p113-BA (a book or film that matters to you), p114-BE (summer cottages).
- **Forced fits, said honestly:**
  - p106-D6 is a phone survey. Use T1 to open and close, and the "how often" lines to answer.
  - p35-BC mixes T16 appearance lines with T18 habits, and its last question is an opinion (T13).
  - p62-R6 is preferences, so add *Det är viktigt för mig att [det finns en park nära].* (It is important to me that there is a park nearby.)
  - p73-BD ends with tips, so borrow T11: *Det är en bra idé att …*
  - p73-BE asks about your last illness at the end: one T15 sentence in the past.

### T19. Compare your home country with Finland

**When to use:**
- Berätta, Din åsikt or Skriva tasks that ask how something is "in your home country" and "here": school, food, healthcare, taxes, parties, clothes, art, daily life, identity.
- In Din åsikt, give your answer to the statement first (T13 step 1). Then use T19 for the reasons.

**The frame:** your link to both countries → the home-country side → the Finland side → one thing that is the same → your view → close.

- Your link:
  - *Jag kommer från [Indien], men jag har bott i Finland i [fyra] år.* (I come from India, but I have lived in Finland for four years.)
  - *Jag vill jämföra [skolan] i mitt hemland och i Finland.* (I want to compare schools in my home country and in Finland.)
- Home-country side: *I mitt hemland [börjar barnen skolan när de är sex].* (In my home country children start school when they are six.)
- Finland side: *…, men i Finland [börjar de när de är sju].* (…, but in Finland they start when they are seven.)
- The rule for both halves: the place comes first, so the verb comes next (G1). *men* does not count as the first part (G10), so *i Finland* is first in the second half and the verb follows it.
- The same: *I båda länderna [är familjen viktig].* (In both countries the family is important.) / *Det är likadant i Finland.* (It is the same in Finland.)
- The biggest difference: *Den största skillnaden är att [skolmaten är gratis här].* (The biggest difference is that school meals are free here.)
- Comparing: *[Maten] är [billigare] i [Indien] än i Finland.* (Food is cheaper in India than in Finland.) The comparative ends in *-are* (taught in full on Day 52).
- Your view:
  - *Jag tycker att [det finska systemet] är bättre, eftersom [___].* (I think the Finnish system is better, because ___.)
  - *Jag saknar [maten] från mitt hemland.* (I miss the food from my home country.)
  - *Det bästa med Finland är att [___].* (The best thing about Finland is that ___.)
- Identity: *Jag känner mig hemma både i [Indien] och i Finland.* (I feel at home both in India and in Finland.)
- Close: *Båda länderna har alltså fördelar och nackdelar.* (So both countries have advantages and disadvantages.) / *Därför tycker jag att [___].* (That is why I think ___.)

**Full example** (Berätta, 1 min prep + 1.5 min). Topic: public transport in your home country and in Finland.

**Short (learn this first, 50 words):** *Jag kommer från Indien, men jag har bott i Finland i fyra år. I Indien åker många människor motorcykel, men i Finland åker man ofta buss eller tåg. I båda länderna är det ganska billigt att åka buss. Jag tycker att kollektivtrafiken är bättre i Finland, eftersom den är tryggare.*

(I come from India, but I have lived in Finland for four years. In India many people go by motorbike, but in Finland people often go by bus or train. In both countries it is quite cheap to take the bus. I think public transport is better in Finland, because it is safer.)

**Expanded (add later):** *Jag kommer från Pune i Indien, men jag har bott i Esbo i fyra år. Jag vill jämföra kollektivtrafiken i mitt hemland och i Finland. I mitt hemland åker många människor buss eller motorcykel, men i Finland åker man ofta buss, tåg eller metro. I Pune är bussarna ofta fulla, och de kommer inte alltid i tid. Här i Esbo går bussarna nästan alltid enligt tidtabellen. Den största skillnaden är att man kan köpa biljetten i en app här. I båda länderna är det ganska billigt att åka buss. En nackdel i Finland är att man måste vänta ute i kylan på vintern. Ändå tycker jag att kollektivtrafiken är bättre i Finland, eftersom den är tryggare och punktligare. Men jag saknar att åka motorcykel med min bror!*

(I come from Pune in India, but I have lived in Espoo for four years. I want to compare public transport in my home country and in Finland. In my home country many people go by bus or motorbike, but in Finland people often go by bus, train or metro. In Pune the buses are often full, and they don't always come on time. Here in Espoo the buses almost always run on schedule. The biggest difference is that you can buy the ticket in an app here. In both countries it is quite cheap to take the bus. One disadvantage in Finland is that you have to wait outside in the cold in winter. Still, I think public transport is better in Finland, because it is safer and more punctual. But I miss riding a motorbike with my brother!)

The expanded version has about 125 words.

**Common errors:**
- *…, men i Finland man åker ofta tåg.* → *…, men i Finland åker man ofta tåg.* (G1: in the second half, *i Finland* is first, so the verb comes next)
- *Maten är mer billig i Indien.* → *Maten är billigare i Indien än i Finland.* (comparative with *-are … än*)

**Required points a rater would look for** [Plan's own]:
- Both countries are named.
- At least two concrete differences, each with an example.
- At least one thing that is the same.
- Your own view with a reason.
- A close.
- In Din åsikt, an answer to the statement in the first sentence.
- In a Skriva opinion text, at least three reasons, as in the official sample [Official sample].

**Book tasks it covers (4):** p88-BA (your home country's school system and Finland's), p52-B4 (restaurants: how often you go, home country versus Finland), p123-BA (your origins and what citizenship means to you), p115-OD (Finnish art compared with art where you grew up).
- **Forced fits, said honestly:**
  - p123-BA is about identity, so it needs the *Jag känner mig hemma både i … och i …* line and T13 reasons more than facts.
  - p52-B4 starts as a habit talk, so use the T18 part 1 "how often" lines first.
- **Also helps** (statements catalogued under T13): p53-O3 (is daily life in Finland easy?), p74-OC (healthcare here), p89-OF (raising children in the Nordic countries), p124-OD (tax levels), p37-OI (how well Finns dress) and p36-OA (are parties here boring?).

### T20. One-offs: five small moves (optional library material)

**Optional since 16 November 2026 (second review).** These situations are rare: 6 of 225 book tasks. Look a move up when a task needs it; you do not have to learn T20 early. The block is not part of Day 30 (its optional slots are given with the block in the last section).

**When to use:** each move fits one kind of task that comes up only once or twice in the practice book. These moves fall outside the top five functions, like T16. Each is short: 3–6 lines. **Use only the move the task asks for.**

**T20a. Resigning politely** (to your boss, face to face)
- *Har du tid en minut? Jag har en sak att berätta.* (Do you have a minute? I have something to tell you.)
- *Jag har fått ett nytt jobb, så jag vill säga upp mig.* (I have got a new job, so I want to resign.) An easier alternative: *så jag vill sluta här*. (so I want to leave.)
- *Min sista arbetsdag blir [den 31 maj].* (My last working day will be 31 May.)
- *Tack för [de här åren]. Jag har lärt mig mycket här.* (Thank you for these years. I have learned a lot here.)
- *Jag kan hjälpa till att lära upp [en ny person].* (I can help train a new person.)

**T20b. Offering things for free**
- *Jag ska flytta i [maj], och jag har saker som jag inte behöver.* (I'm moving in May, and I have things that I don't need.) Inside the *som*-clause, *inte* goes before the verb (G2).
- *Vill du ha [min soffa]? Du får den gratis.* (Do you want my sofa? You can have it for free.)
- *Den är [fem år gammal], men den är i bra skick.* (It is five years old, but it is in good condition. These are the T3 seller lines.)
- *Du måste bara hämta den själv före [den 20 maj].* (You just have to collect it yourself before 20 May.)
- *Säg till om du vill ha något!* (Tell me if you want anything!)

**T20c. Saying you are afraid or worried, and asking for what you need**
- *Förlåt, jag är lite nervös. Jag är rädd för [sprutor].* (Sorry, I'm a bit nervous. I'm afraid of needles.)
- *Kan du berätta vad du ska göra innan du börjar?* (Can you tell me what you are going to do before you start?)
- *Får jag ligga ner? Jag blev yr förra gången.* (May I lie down? I felt dizzy last time.)
- *Tack, det känns bättre nu.* (Thanks, that feels better now.)
- With no listener (bad news about yourself): *Oj nej! [Fem kilo] mer än i [januari]! Nu måste jag börja [röra på mig mer].* (Oh no! Five kilos more than in January! Now I have to start moving more.)

- Why it matters: *Den är viktig för mig eftersom [___].* (It is important to me because ___.)
- Advantages and disadvantages: *En fördel är att [___]. En nackdel är att [___].* (One advantage is that ___. One disadvantage is that ___.)
- Grammar: *där* and *på vintern* first, so the verb comes next (G1). *en stuga – den*, *ett hus – det*, *ett kvarter – det*: the pronoun follows en or ett (G7).

**Why it matters for the exam:** in the practice book, 9 tasks ask you to describe your home, your area, nature, a cottage, a book or your own style [Book count]. The plan's own writing count (section 2.1) already lists "describe a place" among the tasks outside the top five functions.

**How we learn it (15 min):**
- 0–5: Use today's place words. Say where your home is, in 3 sentences: *Mitt hus ligger …, bredvid …, mittemot …*. Then add *Där brukar jag …* and *Det är viktigt för mig eftersom …*.
- 5–15:
  - p12-D1 (pages 12–13): a friend visits your flat. Welcome them, describe the home, give one advantage and one disadvantage. One take, about 20 s per turn [Plan's practice limit].
  - p62-R6 and p62-R7 (page 62): Reagera, 20 s prep + 25 s each.

**Next study days:** Day 39: p64-S2 (page 64), the written message about your area, 15 minutes, with the T18 message points; it replaces the Day 39 writing prompt. Day 40: p62-BA, p113-BA, as Berätta talks. The others (p35-BC, p63-BC, p114-BE) are in the question bank of the book map.

**Done when (Green / Amber / Red):** Green if the dialogue covers welcome, two details of the home, one advantage and one disadvantage, and each Reagera says where it is, what it is like and why it matters, on your own; Amber if a point was weak or you hesitated; Red if you misread a task or needed the model. Target: about 25 seconds per Reagera.

**Typical errors to watch:**
- *Där vi brukar bada.* (said as a sentence on its own) → *Där brukar vi bada.* (G1)
- *Huset är liten.* → *Huset är litet.* (G7)
