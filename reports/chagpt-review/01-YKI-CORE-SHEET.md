## How to use this sheet

- **Learn frames, not whole answers.** A frame is a sentence with a gap [ ] that you fill with the card's details. The example answers show a shape; never learn one as a whole answer.
- **Audio:** hear every line before you say it: from your teacher, a Swedish voice, or the book's QR tracks. For Days 1–14 the single list of lines to hear is `YKI-AUDIO-LIST-DAYS-1-14.md`.
- **On a task card:** underline what it asks for, pick one main answer shape (the "engine", section 2) and borrow single moves from other engines when the card asks for them, open and close the way the situation needs (section 4), and fill the shape.
- **When to stop.** In a call or a message, stop when every point is covered. In an opinion or a story task, a ticked checklist is the minimum, not the finish: develop each point with a reason, an example and a link word (section 2, engine 4).
- **Answer length (practice aims, not exam rules).** Practise at about 80 words a minute: 20 seconds holds about 25–33 words, 25 seconds about 33–40, 1 minute about 80 and 1.5 minutes about 120.
- **Move on by Green, Amber or Red** (section 14), not by counts.
- **With an AI chat:** paste this sheet in and ask it to quiz you from the English. It is not an official rater, and its Swedish can be wrong.

# ACTIVE CORE: recall and use without notes

## 1. Five grammar rules these frames use

1. **The finite verb comes second.** The finite verb is the verb that shows the time (*är, bor, kan, jobbade*). In a main clause it is the second unit, and a unit can be several words (*I dag*, *Förra veckan*, *På fredag klockan tio*). If a time, a place or a word like *tyvärr* (unfortunately) comes first, the subject moves after the verb: *I dag jobbar jag hemma.* (Today I'm working at home.) Not *I dag jag jobbar*.
2. **Questions.** A yes/no question starts with the finite verb: *Har du tid i morgon?* (Do you have time tomorrow?) An ordinary question starts with the question word, then the verb: *Var bor du?* (Where do you live?) When the question word is the subject, the verb follows at once: *Vem kommer?* (Who is coming?) Inside a longer sentence (an indirect question), use subject–verb order: *Jag undrar när kursen börjar.* (I wonder when the course starts.) If the question word is the subject there, add *som*: *Vet du vem som kommer?* (Do you know who is coming?)
3. ***inte* (not)** comes after the finite verb in a main clause: *Jag kan inte komma.* (I can't come.) In a basic subordinate clause (after *att, eftersom, om* and *när*), the subject comes first, then *inte*, then the finite verb: *Problemet är att den inte startar.* (The problem is that it doesn't start.) / *… eftersom jag inte har tid.* (… because I don't have time.) *redan* (already) and *alltid* (always) move the same way.
4. **One verb form for every person:** *jag bor, du bor, vi bor.* Don't use *är* before another verb to make the present tense: *Jag bor i Esbo*, not *Jag är bor i Esbo*. After *kan, vill, måste, ska, skulle* and *brukar* comes the base form, with no *att*: *Jag vill boka en tid.* (I want to book an appointment.)
5. ***den, det, de* (it, it, they).** *den* for an *en*-word (*en cykel*: *Den är tre år gammal.*). *det* for an *ett*-word, and the adjective usually adds *-t* (*ett skrivbord*: *Det är två år gammalt.*); that is a common pattern, not a law (*bra* never changes, and *liten* becomes *litet*). *de* for a plural, with *-a* (*De är tre år gamla.*).

## 2. The six answer shapes ("engines")

Pick one **main** engine per task: it tells you what to say, in what order. Then borrow single moves from the other engines when the card asks for them. A complaint about a repair, for example, runs on Fix, borrows the Story sequence to say what happened (*Först … Sedan … Till slut …*) and an Ask line for the new time (*Har ni en ledig tid på torsdag?*). The situation, not the engine, decides how you open and close (section 4).

### Engine 1: Ask (request, booking, information)

**Shape:** who I am → what I want → one or two questions → confirm → thanks.

**Example, a call to book an appointment (Day 4's lines; the parts in "…" are the other voice):**
*Hej, det här är Alex Kumar. Jag skulle vilja boka en tid.* … *Jag kan inte komma på morgonen. Kan jag komma efter klockan fyra?* … *Tack för hjälpen! Hej då!*
(Hi, this is Alex Kumar. I would like to book an appointment. … I can't come in the morning. Can I come after four? … Thanks for the help! Bye!)

The price question (*Hur mycket kostar besöket?*, How much does the visit cost?) and the confirm line (*Okej, då säger vi …*) join the call on Day 12.

### Engine 2: Fix (complaint, repair, asking someone to stop)

**Shape:** what is wrong → since when → why it matters → what I want → close.

**Example, a call to a repair firm (an official sample type):**
*Hej, det här är Alex Kumar. Jag ringer angående min tvättmaskin. Den är fortfarande trasig. En reparatör var här i tisdags, men den fungerar fortfarande inte. Vi är fyra personer och vi behöver tvättmaskinen varje dag. Kan ni komma i morgon eller på torsdag?*
(Hi, this is Alex Kumar. I'm calling about my washing machine. It is still broken. A repair person was here last Tuesday, but it still doesn't work. We are four people and we need the washing machine every day. Can you come tomorrow or on Thursday?)

### Engine 3: Social (react, thank, apologise, invite, refuse)

**Shape:** react or say the news → one detail or reason → a question, an offer or a new time → close.

**Example, a 20-second voicemail saying you will be late (an official sample type; about 32 words):**
*Hej Sara, det är Alex. Förlåt, jag blir ungefär en kvart försenad, eftersom bussen inte har kommit. Jag är där kvart över sex. Kan du vänta på mig vid ingången? Vi ses!*
(Hi Sara, it's Alex. Sorry, I'll be about a quarter of an hour late, because the bus hasn't come. I'll be there at quarter past six. Can you wait for me at the entrance? See you!)

### Engine 4: Opinion (your view, and advice)

**Shape:** view → reason + example → reason → the other side → weigh the two sides → conclusion. For advice: *Jag tycker att du ska …* + a reason + an example.

**Example, a spoken opinion (*Det är bättre att bo i en stad än på landet. Håller du med?*):**
*Jag tycker att det är bättre att bo i en stad. För det första är det lättare att leva utan bil. Till exempel går bussar och tåg ofta. För det andra finns det många jobb i staden. Å andra sidan är det dyrt att bo i en stad. Men för mig är fördelarna större än nackdelarna. Därför tycker jag att det är bättre att bo i en stad.*
(I think it is better to live in a city. First, it is easier to live without a car. For example, buses and trains run often. Second, there are many jobs in the city. On the other hand, it is expensive to live in a city. But for me the advantages are bigger than the disadvantages. That is why I think it is better to live in a city.)

**Weigh before you conclude.** A *Därför* straight after a disadvantage does not follow from it. Say which side wins for you (*Men för mig är fördelarna större än nackdelarna.*), then conclude.

**Develop, don't stop at the tick.** In opinion and story tasks the checklist is the minimum. Give each reason an example and a link word:
- Bare: *Jag tycker att det är bättre att bo i en stad. Det är lättare utan bil.* (I think it is better to live in a city. It is easier without a car.)
- Developed: *Jag tycker att det är bättre att bo i en stad, eftersom det är lättare att leva utan bil. Till exempel går bussarna ofta, så jag kan ta bussen till jobbet varje dag.* (I think it is better to live in a city, because it is easier to live without a car. For example, the buses run often, so I can take the bus to work every day.)

In speaking, two developed reasons are enough. A letter to the editor (an official sample type) opens with the topic (*Jag vill skriva om …*) and needs at least three reasons (*För det tredje …*).

### Engine 5: Describe (a person, a habit, a place, something you sell)

**Shape:** what or who → two details → what I usually do → why it matters. As a seller: age, condition, price, a time to come, your address.

**Example, a talk about a person important to you (an official sample type):**
*Jag vill berätta om min vän Ravi. Vi har känt varandra i femton år. Ravi är lång och har kort svart hår. Vi brukar prata i telefon en gång i veckan. Han är viktig för mig eftersom han alltid finns där när jag behöver honom.*
(I want to tell you about my friend Ravi. We have known each other for fifteen years. Ravi is tall and has short black hair. We usually talk on the phone once a week. He is important to me because he is always there when I need him.)

This is the core of a 1.5-minute talk (about 45 words, so about a third of the time); develop each point (section 2, engine 4) until the time is used.

### Engine 6: Story (what happened, and reports)

**Shape:** when and where → first, then, in the end → how it felt → now. For a report to an office or the police, add a description and your contact details.

**Example, a message explaining why you were late:**
*Hej Sara! Förlåt att jag kom så sent i går. Först kom bussen för sent, och sedan missade jag tåget. Till slut tog jag en taxi, men det var mycket trafik. Det var jobbigt, eftersom jag ville vara med från början. Kram, Alex*
(Hi Sara! Sorry I came so late yesterday. First the bus came too late, and then I missed the train. In the end I took a taxi, but there was a lot of traffic. It was hard, because I wanted to be there from the start. Hugs, Alex)

## 3. The seven official sample tasks: what each must contain

Tick each point. In practice a forgotten point makes the task Amber (section 14); the booking gate (plan 4.7) needs every point.

1. **Talk about a person important to you** (speaking: 1 minute to prepare, 1.5 minutes to talk). The plan's six points: who; how long you have known them; looks and personality; what you do together; why they matter; a memory or a wish.
2. **A buyer phones about your "for sale" ad** (speaking: turns of about 10–25 seconds). Answer the questions (still for sale? age? condition? price? a lower offer?), suggest a time and say where you live.
3. **You phone a repair firm about something still broken** (speaking: 6 turns). Who you are; what is broken; that it is still broken after a repair visit, and when; a protest with a reason; what you want (a new time, your address).
4. **A voicemail saying you will be late** (speaking: 20 seconds). Who you are; that you will be late; a short reason; when you will be there.
5. **An e-mail to a friend about an event** (writing). What; when; where; two more details; a proper opening and closing. The plan adds an invitation question (*Vill du följa med?*).
6. **A letter to the editor** (writing). The topic; your opinion; at least three reasons. The plan adds an example, the other side and a conclusion that matches your opinion.
7. **A complaint about a bill** (writing, formal). Which bill (number, date and amount); at least two reasons why it is too high; an amount you think is fair; your contact details; a formal opening and closing.

## 4. Openings and closings: they depend on the situation

| Situation | Open | Close |
|---|---|---|
| Phone call | *Hej, det här är [namn]. Jag ringer angående [___].* (Hi, this is … I'm calling about …) | *Okej, då säger vi [torsdag klockan tio].* (OK, then we'll say Thursday at ten; only to confirm what you heard.) *Tack för hjälpen. Hej då!* (Thanks for the help. Bye!) |
| Informal e-mail or message | *Hej [namn]!* (Hi …!) | *Hälsningar, [namn]* (Regards, …) |
| Formal e-mail or letter | *Hej! Jag skriver angående [___].* (Hi! I am writing about …; *om* works too) | *Ni når mig på [telefonnummer] eller [e-postadress]. Med vänliga hälsningar, [för- och efternamn]* (You can reach me on … or … Kind regards, …) |
| Opinion (talk or text) | *Jag tycker att [___].* (I think that …) | *Därför tycker jag att [___].* (That is why I think that …) |
| Story or describe (talk) | *Jag vill berätta om [___].* (I want to tell you about …) | *Det var allt jag ville säga.* (That was all I wanted to say.) |
| Face-to-face request | *Ursäkta, kan du [___]?* (Excuse me, can you …?) More polite: *skulle du kunna* | *Tack!* (Thanks!) |
| Reacting to news | *Vad roligt!* (How nice!) / *Vad tråkigt att höra!* (I'm sorry to hear that!) | A question or an offer (section 7) |

*Hej!* is the default formal opening. *Bästa mottagare,* is a reference alternative (section 13).

Never open a hobby talk with a phone greeting, and never close it with a formal letter closing.

## 5. The core frames (about 60)

About 60 frame cards. A few cards hold two sentences (the seller lines, the advice line); on the day you learn them, each sentence counts as one new pattern (load rule 1, section 14). Lines in several groups are listed once per group (see "The counts overlap" at the top).

**Me (6)**
- *Jag heter [___].* (My name is …)
- *Jag kommer från [___].* (I come from …)
- *Jag bor i [___].* (I live in …)
- *Jag talar [___].* (I speak …)
- *Jag lär mig svenska.* (I am learning Swedish.)
- *Jag jobbar som [___].* (I work as …)

**Phone frame (4) and letter frame (5):** in section 4.

**Ask (6)**
- *Jag skulle vilja [boka en tid].* (I would like to book an appointment.)
- *Har ni en ledig tid [på tisdag]?* (Do you have a free time on Tuesday?)
- *Passar det [på torsdag klockan tio]?* (Does Thursday at ten suit you?)
- *Hur mycket kostar [besöket]?* (How much does the visit cost?)
- *Jag undrar om [det finns lediga platser].* (I wonder if there are free places.)
- *När kan jag [komma]?* (When can I come?)

**Fix (8)**
- *[Den] är fortfarande trasig.* (It is still broken.)
- *Problemet är att [den inte startar].* (The problem is that it doesn't start.)
- *En reparatör var här [i tisdags], men det hjälpte inte.* (A repair person was here last Tuesday, but it didn't help.)
- *Kan ni [komma i morgon eller på torsdag]?* (Can you come tomorrow or on Thursday?)
- *Jag vill [få pengarna tillbaka].* (I want to get my money back.)
- *Fakturan är för hög av två skäl.* (The bill is too high, for two reasons.)
- *Jag tycker att [___] euro är ett rimligt belopp.* (I think … euros is a reasonable amount.)
- *Ursäkta, skulle du kunna [prata lite tystare]?* (Excuse me, could you talk a bit more quietly?)

**Social (7)**
- *Tack för [inbjudan].* (Thanks for the invitation.)
- *Tyvärr kan jag inte [komma], eftersom [jag jobbar].* (Unfortunately I can't come, because I'm working.)
- *Kan vi ses [på lördag] i stället?* (Can we meet on Saturday instead?)
- *Förlåt, jag blir [en kvart] försenad, eftersom [___].* (Sorry, I'll be a quarter of an hour late, because …)
- *Jag är där [kvart över sex].* (I'll be there at quarter past six.)
- *Det är [på lördag] klockan [___] [i/på ___].* (It is on Saturday at … in/at …)
- *Vill du [följa med]?* (Do you want to come along?)

**Opinion (8)**
- *Jag tycker att [___].* (I think that …)
- *Jag håller med.* / *Jag håller inte med.* (I agree. / I don't agree.)
- *För det första [___]. För det andra [___]. För det tredje [___].* (First … Second … Third … The verb comes next: *För det första sparar man tid.*)
- *Till exempel [___].* (For example …)
- *Å andra sidan [___].* (On the other hand …)
- *Men för mig är fördelarna större än nackdelarna.* (But for me the advantages are bigger than the disadvantages. Say it before the conclusion.)
- *Därför tycker jag att [___].* (That is why I think that …)
- *Jag tycker att du ska [___]. Du kan till exempel [___].* (I think you should … You can for example … For advice.)

**Describe (11)**
- A person:
  - *Jag vill berätta om [___].* (I want to tell you about …)
  - *Hon är [snäll och glad].* (She is kind and cheerful.)
  - *Jag har känt henne [i tio år].* (I have known her for ten years.)
  - *Vi brukar [laga mat tillsammans].* (We usually cook together.)
  - *Hon är viktig för mig eftersom [___].* (She is important to me because …)
- A habit, a place or a thing:
  - *Jag brukar [simma två gånger i veckan].* (I usually swim twice a week.)
  - *Det finns [en bastu och en brygga].* (There is a sauna and a jetty.)
  - *Den ligger [nära centrum].* (It is near the centre. With an *ett*-word: *Det ligger …*)
- Something you sell:
  - *Ja, den finns kvar. Den är [tre] år gammal och i bra skick.* (Yes, it's still for sale. It is three years old and in good condition.)
  - *Den kostar [150] euro, men du kan få den för [130] euro.* (It costs 150 euros, but you can have it for 130.)
  - *Du kan komma [i morgon efter klockan sex]. Jag bor på [Solvägen 5] i [Esbo].* (You can come tomorrow after six. I live at Solvägen 5 in Espoo.)

**Story (5)**
- *[Förra veckan] hände något [konstigt].* (Last week something strange happened.)
- *Först [___]. Sedan [___]. Till slut [___].* (First … Then … In the end … The verb comes next after each.)
- *Det var [jobbigt], eftersom [___].* (It was hard, because …)
- *Jag har tappat bort [min plånbok].* (I have lost my wallet.)
- *Det hände [i går kväll] [på bussen].* (It happened last night on the bus.)

For a report, borrow *Kan ni [kontakta mig]?* (Can you contact me?) from Fix.

## 6. Twelve reason pairs

Each pair has a "for" and an "against" side, so you can argue either way. Follow a reason with *Till exempel …* and a detail from the task. Use only reasons that fit.

| Topic | For (the positive side) | Against (the negative side) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | *Det sparar tid.* (It saves time.) | *Det tar mycket tid.* (It takes a lot of time.) |
| Cost | *Det är billigare.* (It is cheaper.) | *Det är dyrt.* (It is expensive.) |
| Health | *Det är bra för hälsan.* (It is good for your health.) | *Man rör sig för lite.* (You move too little.) |
| Environment | *Det är bra för miljön.* (It is good for the environment.) | *Det är dåligt för miljön.* (It is bad for the environment.) |
| Stress | *Man blir mindre stressad.* (You get less stressed.) | *Man blir lätt stressad.* (You easily get stressed.) |
| People | *Man träffar nya människor.* (You meet new people.) | *Man kan känna sig ensam.* (You can feel lonely.) |
| Safety | *Det är tryggt.* (It feels safe.) | *Det kan vara farligt.* (It can be dangerous.) |
| Choice | *Man kan välja själv.* (You can choose yourself.) | *Det passar inte alla.* (It does not suit everyone.) |
| Ease | *Det gör vardagen enklare.* (It makes everyday life easier.) | *Det kan vara svårt för äldre människor.* (It can be hard for older people.) |
| Children | *Det är bra för barnen.* (It is good for the children.) | *Barnen sitter för mycket framför skärmen.* (Children sit too much in front of the screen.) |
| Quality | *Kvaliteten är ofta bättre.* (The quality is often better.) | *Servicen blir sämre.* (The service gets worse.) |
| Flexibility | *Man kan göra det när som helst.* (You can do it at any time.) | *Det kan bli fel.* (Things can go wrong.) |

*man* means "you" or "people in general". After *En nackdel är att* (a disadvantage is that), *inte* moves before the verb: *En nackdel är att det inte passar alla.*

## 7. Ten reactions

Shape: react → one detail → a question, an offer or a close. [bank N] is the plan's number. Example: *Grattis! Vad roligt att du har fått jobbet! När börjar du?* (Congratulations! How nice that you got the job! When do you start?)

1. *Grattis! Vad roligt!* (Congratulations! How nice!) [bank 1]
2. *Vad tråkigt att höra.* (I'm sorry to hear that.) [bank 6]
3. *Krya på dig!* (Get well soon! Only for illness.) [bank 8]
4. *Jag beklagar sorgen.* (I'm sorry for your loss. Only for a death.) [bank 12]
5. *Tack så mycket för hjälpen!* (Thank you very much for the help!) [bank 14]
6. *Det gör inget.* (It doesn't matter. The reply to an apology.) [bank 19]
7. *Förlåt att jag är sen.* (Sorry I'm late. When you are already late.) [bank 22]
8. *Förlåt, jag blir lite sen.* (Sorry, I'll be a bit late. Before the meeting time.) [bank 23]
9. *Ja, gärna!* (Yes, please! / Yes, I'd like to! A warm yes to an offer or an invitation.) [bank 25]
10. *Tyvärr kan jag inte.* (Unfortunately I can't.) [bank 28]

## 8. Eight rescue phrases

Never go silent, and never switch to English.

1. *Förlåt, jag menar …* (Sorry, I mean … Correct yourself once, then carry on.)
2. *Jag säger det på ett annat sätt.* (I'll say it another way.)
3. *Jag kommer inte på ordet, men det är något man använder för att …* (I can't think of the word, but it's something you use to … For example, if *bromsarna* (the brakes) is the missing word: *… något man använder för att stanna.* (… something you use to stop.) Describe the use with words you know.)
4. *Det är som en …* (It's like a …)
5. *Jag är inte säker, men jag tror att …* (I'm not sure, but I think that …)
6. *Det beror på …* (It depends on …)
7. *Till exempel …* (For example …)
8. *Det var allt jag ville säga.* (That was all I wanted to say.)

**If you miss what the recorded voice said.** You cannot ask it to repeat. Choose one of three moves:
1. **You understood all of it:** answer it.
2. **You understood part of it:** answer that part.
3. **You missed it:** give a relevant point from your card, without agreeing to anything: *Jag kan komma på fredag efter klockan fyra.* (I can come on Friday after four.)

Do not say *okej* or *ja* by reflex: it can agree to something you did not hear.

## 9. Thirty verbs, in three steps

**Step 1: the present of all 30** (base form → present).

*vara → är* (be), *ha → har* (have), *göra → gör* (do, make), *gå → går* (go, walk), *komma → kommer* (come), *åka → åker* (go, travel), *få → får* (get, may), *ta → tar* (take), *ge → ger* (give), *se → ser* (see), *säga → säger* (say), *veta → vet* (know), *kunna → kan* (can), *vilja → vill* (want), *bli → blir* (become, get), *köpa → köper* (buy), *betala → betalar* (pay), *ringa → ringer* (phone), *skicka → skickar* (send), *boka → bokar* (book), *jobba → jobbar* (work), *bo → bor* (live), *träffa → träffar* (meet), *fungera → fungerar* (work, of a thing), *hjälpa → hjälper* (help), *skriva → skriver* (write), *äta → äter* (eat), *sova → sover* (sleep), *glömma → glömmer* (forget), *tycka → tycker* (think, an opinion).

**Step 2: the past of 12.** *var* (was), *köpte* (bought), *ringde* (phoned), *kom* (came), *glömde* (forgot), *gick* (went), *hjälpte* (helped), *tog* (took), *blev* (became, got), *fick* (got), *sa* (said), *bodde* (lived). Use the past with a finished time: *Jag köpte den i måndags.* (I bought it last Monday.) Phase 1 teaches only 5 of them, in sentences: *var, kom, gick* (Day 6) and *ringde, köpte* (Day 9). The other 7 come in Phase 2: *tog, glömde, blev, hjälpte* on Day 34a, and *fick, sa, bodde* from the plan's verb table (2.9) when a task needs them.

**The future** (plan 2.9, G8) is library material in Phase 1, with one exception: Day 9 learns *ska* + base form as a chunk: *I morgon ska jag ringa till banken.* (Tomorrow I'm going to call the bank.)

**Step 3: the perfect, only inside chunks.** Learn these whole: *Jag har bott i Finland i fem år.* (I have lived in Finland for five years.) / *Jag har redan betalat.* (I have already paid.) / *Har ni fått mitt mejl?* (Have you got my e-mail?)

# RECOGNITION CORE: understand when you hear or read it

## 10. Words to recognise on the task card

**Instruction words (23):** *läs* (read), *lyssna* (listen), *välj* (choose), *skriv* (write), *svara* (answer), *fråga* (ask), *berätta* (tell), *förklara* (explain), *beskriv* (describe), *jämför* (compare), *föreslå* (suggest), *motivera* (give reasons for), *rätt / fel* (true / false), *åtminstone* (at least), *minst* (at least: *minst tre skäl*, at least three reasons), *ange* (state, give: *Ange ditt telefonnummer.*), *be om* (ask for: *Be om en ny tid.*), *ställ två frågor* (ask two questions), *ge exempel* (give examples), *nämn* (name, mention), *ta ställning* (take a position: say whether you agree), *för- och nackdelar* (advantages and disadvantages), *lämna ett meddelande* (leave a message).

**Two lists, not one.** These 23 are the full recognition list. Days 1–13 teach 20 of them; the other 3 (*ge exempel, ta ställning, för- och nackdelar*) are proposed for the opinion days (Days 29–31b). The Day 14 word test (E1) is a separate list of exactly 41 words: the 20 instruction words met by Day 13, the 11 question words below, and 10 meaning-flip words (*inte* not, *tyvärr* unfortunately, *i stället* instead, *stängt* closed, *men* but, *inställd* cancelled, *försenad* delayed, *redan* already, *flyttad* moved, *ändrad* changed).

**Question words (11):** *vem* (who), *vad* (what), *var* (where), *när* (when), *hur* (how), *hur mycket* (how much), *hur många* (how many), *hur länge* (how long), *hur ofta* (how often), *varför* (why), *vilken / vilket / vilka* (which).

**The question-to-answer reflex.** Hear the question word, know what the answer must hold. The table is built up from Day 2 (three rows), one row on the day each question word is taught; *Vad hände?* comes in Phase 2 (Day 34a). The full set:

| Question | Your answer gives, and how it starts |
|---|---|
| *Var?* | a place: *I [Esbo]. / På [jobbet].* |
| *När?* | a time: *På [fredag]. / Klockan [tio].* |
| *Varför?* | a reason: *Eftersom [jag jobbar].* |
| *Hur mycket?* | a price or amount: *[Sextio] euro.* |
| *Hur länge?* | a duration: *I [två år]. / Sedan [maj].* |
| *Vad tycker du?* | an opinion: *Jag tycker att [___].* |
| *Vad hände?* | a past event: *[I går] hände något [oväntat].* (Yesterday something unexpected happened.) |
| *Vad vill du?* | a request: *Jag skulle vilja [___].* |

## 11. One answer, several questions

Learn one answer; recognise the different ways the question can be asked. The curriculum adds these a few at a time in Phase 1.

- *Jag bor i Esbo.* answers *Var bor du?* / *Var bor du nu?* / *I vilken stad bor du?* / *Var någonstans bor du?* (Where do you live? / … now? / In which town …? / Whereabouts …?)
- *Jag kommer från Indien.* answers *Var kommer du ifrån?* / *Varifrån kommer du?* / *Vilket land kommer du från?* (Where are you from? / … / Which country are you from?)
- *Jag heter Alex.* answers *Vad heter du?* / *Vad är ditt namn?* (What is your name?)

# REFERENCE: look up when a task needs it

## 12. The substitution bank: start verb + action

A look-up table, not a list to memorise. Take a start verb, add an action in the base form (no *att*), and make it positive, negative or a question.

| Start verb | Yes | No (*inte* after the start verb) | Question (start verb first) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *kan* (can) | *Jag kan [komma].* | *Jag kan inte [komma].* | *Kan du [komma]?* |
| *vill* (want to) | *Jag vill [komma].* | *Jag vill inte [komma].* | *Vill du [komma]?* |
| *måste* (must, have to) | *Jag måste [jobba hemma].* | *Jag måste inte [jobba hemma].* | *Måste du [jobba hemma]?* |
| *ska* (am going to) | *Jag ska [ringa].* | *Jag ska inte [ringa].* | *Ska du [ringa]?* |
| *brukar* (usually) | *Jag brukar [jobba hemma].* | *Jag brukar inte [jobba hemma].* | *Brukar du [jobba hemma]?* |

**Ten actions for the gap:** *komma* (come), *ringa* (phone), *jobba hemma* (work at home), *boka en tid* (book an appointment), *betala med kort* (pay by card), *träffa dig* (meet you), *hjälpa dig* (help you), *skicka ett mejl* (send an e-mail), *åka till jobbet* (go to work), *köpa en ny* (buy a new one).

- *måste inte* means "don't have to" (*behöver inte* says the same). For "must not", say *får inte*: *Du får inte parkera här.* (You must not park here.)
- With a time first, the start verb is still second: *I morgon kan jag inte komma.* (I can't come tomorrow.)
- Not every pairing makes sense (*Jag brukar köpa en ny*); use the ones that do.

## 13. Techniques and alternatives (no new lines to learn)

**Concept words: a way to find a reason fast.** Ask what the statement touches: *tid* (time), *pengar* (money), *hälsa* (health), *miljö* (environment), *trygghet* (safety), *människor* (people), *barn* (children), *arbete* (work), *frihet* (freedom), *stress*, *kvalitet* (quality), *problem*. Then take the matching pair in section 6. Working from home? *tid* and *stress*: *Det sparar tid. Man blir mindre stressad.*

**Sentence families.** Learn a sentence with 2–3 variants, never a verb as a four-form table.
- Time, money, work: *Det tar mycket tid.* (It takes a lot of time.) / *Det kostar mycket pengar.* (It costs a lot of money.) / *Det är mycket arbete.* (It is a lot of work.)
- Yesterday, today, tomorrow: *I går jobbade jag hemma.* (Yesterday I worked at home.) / *I dag jobbar jag hemma.* (Today I'm working at home.) / *I morgon ska jag jobba hemma.* (Tomorrow I'm going to work at home.) Up to now: *Jag har jobbat hemma i två år.* (I have worked at home for two years.)
- With a future time word, the present works too: *På måndag jobbar jag hemma.* or *På måndag ska jag jobba hemma.* (On Monday I'm working at home.)

**Word recovery** (the long form of rescue phrases 3 and 4): *Det är en sak som [___].* (It's a thing that …) / *Det är en plats där [___].* (It's a place where …) / *Det är en person som [___].* (It's a person who …) / *Man använder den för att [___].* (You use it to …) / *Det är ungefär som [___].* (It's roughly like …)

**Formal opening, alternative.** *Bästa mottagare,* (Dear recipient,) is used in Finland in formal letters. *Hej!* (section 4) is the default and is always safe.

# STUDY RULES (not language to learn)

## 14. The nine daily load rules, and when to move on

1. **At most 6 new patterns and 8 new cards a day.** Count patterns, not bullets or cards: a pattern is one sentence shape you can recall on its own. A card that holds 2–3 sentences counts as 2–3 patterns. A pattern you already know, or a simple swap of a detail in it, is not new.
2. One grammar point a day, stated in one sentence.
3. Review stops at 8 minutes, finished or not.
4. Hear each new line before saying it.
5. One speaking task and one typed task. A book task replaces the day's own prompt.
6. A day is 60 minutes. If a block is added, another is removed.
7. A day is done when a changed version of the task works, not when a list is recited fast.
8. Mark the day Green, Amber or Red (below). Only Red repeats the skill tomorrow, with new details.
9. If a day holds more than rule 1 allows, split it into day a and day b. Day b uses the same tasks with new details.

**Listening and reading every day.** A 60-minute day gives 8 minutes to Finland-Swedish listening or reading, and a 90-minute day gives 10 (plan section 7). Light, mock and test days set their own amount, and their day entry says how much.

**Green, Amber, Red.**
- **Green, move on:** you understood the task and gave every required point on your own (the model only to check afterwards).
- **Amber, move on with revision:** understandable, but you hesitated, repeated a mistake or forgot a point. Put the top 1–2 mistakes on the error list and revisit them later. Do not repeat the day.
- **Red, repeat the skill tomorrow with new details:** you misunderstood the question, could not answer, or needed the model to answer.
- **Borderline cases.** A forgotten required point (contact details, an amount) in ordinary practice is Amber, unless the message cannot be used without it; then it is Red. Small grammar slips, or a wrong tense the listener still understands (*I går ringer jag till banken.*, present for past), are Amber or Green, never Red on their own.
- Counts (errors, words, seconds, "4 of 5") are targets, never gates. Spelling and capitals never make a task Amber or Red if the message is clear. Only the plan's booking gate (4.7) is a fixed test, and it needs every required point.

**Time pressure grows step by step.** First hearing and repeating: no timer. Saying it from the English: gentle (double the target time). Changing the sentence: moderate. A question you have not practised: exam-like. Mocks: full exam conditions.

**The transfer test for any new sentence** (*Jag bor i Esbo.*): 1 recall it from the English; 2 swap a detail (*i Vanda*); 3 change the person (*Hon bor i Vanda.*); 4 understand the question it answers (*Var bor du?*); 5 build that question yourself from the statement (*Jag bor i Esbo.* → *Var bor du?*; *Och du?* is a useful move too, but it does not test this); 6 understand it spoken; 7 type it.

**Active recall.**
- **Cover and say:** cover the Swedish, read the English, say the Swedish aloud, then uncover and check. A miss comes back tomorrow.
- **Swap drill:** say one frame three times with a new detail each time: *Kan vi ses [på lördag] i stället?* → *på söndag*, *i morgon*, *efter jobbet*.

## 15. The first two days

`YKI-LEARNER-DAYS-1-14.md` is the full version of these days; where it differs from this summary, the learner pages win.

### Day 1: say who you are

**New frames (5):** *Jag heter [Alex].* (My name is …) / *Jag kommer från [Indien].* (I come from …) / *Jag bor i [Esbo].* (I live in …) / *Jag talar [engelska, hindi och marathi].* (I speak …) / *Jag lär mig svenska.* (I am learning Swedish.)

**Questions to understand (4):** *Vad heter du?* (What is your name?) / *Var kommer du ifrån?* (Where are you from?) / *Var bor du?* (Where do you live?) / *Vilka språk talar du?* (Which languages do you speak?)

**Instruction words to recognise (2):** *berätta* (tell), *skriv* (write).

**The one grammar fact:** the verb has the same form for every person: *jag bor, du bor*.

**Steps (60 minutes):** hear each frame three times and repeat it (no timer) → cover and say each frame from the English, then swap the detail three times → answer the four questions, mixed, in full sentences → speaking card *Berätta om dig själv.* (Tell about yourself.): record five sentences, listen once, redo once → set up å, ä and ö, and type the five sentences from memory → change check: answer as Sara (from Sweden, lives in Åbo, speaks Swedish and Finnish; the words you need: *Sverige* (Sweden), *Åbo* (Turku), *svenska och finska* (Swedish and Finnish)) → 8 cards; start an error log.

**Model:** *Hej! Jag heter Alex. Jag kommer från Indien. Jag bor i Esbo. Jag talar engelska, hindi och marathi. Jag lär mig svenska.*

**Done when (section 14):** Green if you understand all four questions and answer each in a full sentence without reading. Spelling slips do not count.

### Day 2: say more, and ask back

**New frames (5):** *Jag jobbar som [ingenjör].* (I work as …) / *Jag har bott i Finland i [fem] år.* (I have lived in Finland for … years. Learn it as one piece.) / *Vad heter du?* (now you ask it) / *Var bor du?* (now you ask it) / *Och du?* (And you?)

**Questions to understand (2 more):** *Vad jobbar du med?* (What do you do for work?) / *Hur länge har du bott i Finland?* (How long have you lived in Finland?)

**Numbers 0–12:** *noll, ett, två, tre, fyra, fem, sex, sju, åtta, nio, tio, elva, tolv.*

**Instruction words to recognise (2):** *fråga* (ask), *svara* (answer).

**The one grammar fact:** in a question, the verb comes straight after the question word: *Var bor du?*, not *Var du bor?*

**Steps (60 minutes):** say the Day 1 cards aloud → hear and repeat the frames and numbers 0–12 → turn answers into questions (*Jag bor i Esbo.* → *Var bor du?*), then answer all six questions → speaking: a helper asks about your name, home, work and years in Finland; answer each in one sentence, and twice add *Och du?*; record, listen once, redo once → typing card: *Skriv till en ny kurskamrat. Berätta om dig själv och ställ två frågor.* (Write to a new course-mate. Tell about yourself and ask two questions.) → hear 8 numbers from 0 to 12 and write the digits → at most 8 cards.

**Model:** *Hej! Jag heter Alex och jag kommer från Indien. Jag bor i Esbo. Jag har bott i Finland i fem år. Jag jobbar som ingenjör. Jag lär mig svenska. Vad heter du? Var bor du? Hälsningar, Alex*

**Done when (section 14):** Green if you answer the six questions in full sentences, your own two questions have the verb before *du*, and the message has a greeting, five facts, two questions and a closing.
