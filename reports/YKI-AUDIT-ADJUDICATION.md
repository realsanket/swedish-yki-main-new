# Adjudication of the ChatGPT audit of the exam-first course

*16 November 2026. Tests the audit's 13 claims against our four files. No existing file was edited.*

**Short version.** The audit is right about the main thing: the course is too big, and too much of it is marked as must-learn. It is wrong or generic in several details, and its first-layer core leaves out the lines that five of the seven official sample tasks need. The fix is to keep our material, shrink what the learner memorises, and cap the daily load.

File abbreviations: **P** = `YKI-EXAM-FIRST-PLAN.md`, **C** = `YKI-EXAM-FIRST-CURRICULUM.md`, **B** = `YKI-BOOK-MAP.md`, **T** = `YKI-NEW-TEMPLATES-T17-T20.md`. Numbers after them are line numbers.

## 2. Evidence per claim

### Claim 1: Day 1 is too hard (CORRECT, with one caveat)

P 1229–1287 asks for all of this in 60 minutes: grammar point G1, 12 chunks, a ladder that already uses *eftersom*, numbers 0–20, a 60-second talk with 8 points, 8 typed sentences, a notice, and cards. The audit's list is accurate.

- Day 1 breaks our own rules. P 1238 makes 33 cards (12 chunks + 21 numbers) in 3 minutes. P 723 allows 15–20 new cards a day.
- The "New" block is 10 minutes in P 667 but 30 minutes on Day 1 (P 1231–1234).
- Chunk 5 (*Jag har bott …*) uses the perfect, which is taught on Day 6.

**Caveat.** The audit assumes a learner with no Swedish at all. P 83 says the learner has had about 51 lessons and an unknown level, and Day 0 decides the starting point. Day 1 is still too heavy for anyone that Day 0 sends to it.

### Claim 2: Days 6 and 9 (CORRECT)

- C 190 and C 208: 15 verbs in four forms, ordinals to 31 and 12 months, in a 10-minute block.
- C 218: "15 verbs in four forms in under 2 minutes with no more than 2 errors".
- C 296: "verbs 1–30 in four forms in under 4 minutes with no more than 3 errors". That is 120 forms, as the audit says.

Both days already hold the better test: a talk with "at least 5 different past forms" (C 214) and "at least 6 past forms" (C 292). The recitation gates add load and prove nothing about speaking.

### Claim 4: Cards (CORRECT)

P 714–723: each card returns 5 times (after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days). After Day 30 that is 5 reviews a day for every daily new card. 15 new cards give 75 reviews a day and 20 give 100. Our cap is 60 reviews in an 8-minute block (P 666), which is 8 seconds a card. A template card ("say every line of T5", P 727) takes far longer. At about 12 seconds a card, 8 minutes holds 40 reviews, which supports 8 new cards a day. The audit's 5–8 is right.

### Claim 5: Readiness criteria (PARTLY)

- "Invented": true, and disclosed. P 1778 is headed "[Plan's own rules]", and P 772 says the A–D scale is not official.
- "Too strict": partly. Rating B means "understandable with some errors" (P 777), which matches the official tolerance in P 50. Registration is binding (P 1086), so a safety margin is sensible.
- The weak part is "no more than 2 engine errors per text" (P 1784). A 150-word letter and a 50-word message get the same allowance. Keep it as a number to track, not as a gate.

### Claim 6: Six engines (PARTLY: adapt, do not adopt as written)

**What is right.** Our 20 IDs overlap. T2 and T12 share lines. T4, T5 and T17 are one move. T6–T9 are one social family. T14, T15, T18 and T19 are one telling family. P 33 and P 738 still say "16 templates"; T 314 makes it 20.

## 3. Adopt, adapt, reject

**Adopt**
1. Cap new cards at 8 a day, and make cards for at most 3 errors a day.
2. Simplify Day 1 and Day 2 (section 5).
3. Remove the verb-recitation gates on Days 6 and 9. Test past forms through the talk.
4. Hear a model before saying any new line.
5. Fix the Day 31 success rule.
6. Reword "one verb only" and the *eftersom* rule.
7. The three Swedish improvements: *Tack, jag kommer gärna!*; *Jag har tappat bort min plånbok.*; *vad du ska göra*.
8. Relabel the 49 book tasks, with the three changes in claim 9.
9. A book task replaces a prompt; it is never added on top.

**Adapt**
1. **Six engines:** use them as the way to choose an answer shape, and keep two frames (phone, letter) and the seven official sample checklists. See section 4.
2. **Reasons and reactions:** 12 reason pairs, as proposed. 10 reactions, not 8, because the audit's list has no reply to an apology and no plain yes.
3. **Booking gate:** keep "every required point" and the outside rating. Drop the error count per text as a gate.
4. **Decoder:** start it on Day 1 with two instruction words a day and the "underline the points" habit.
5. **Unseen prompts:** do not build separate packs. Treat every printed prompt as seen practice. Use fresh prompts for the mocks that count (see section 8).
6. **Day 0:** do the three short checks first (instruction words, numbers, engine test: 18 minutes, P 896–918). Do the long parts only if those pass.
7. **T18 routine talk:** move it into the first two weeks.

**Reject**
1. Choosing or re-testing the subtest pair now, and a four-skill diagnostic. The learner has said this is decided later.
2. A new data model with a dozen fields per task, and five new files. This is building, not studying.
3. Replacing the A–D rating with a six-part analytic sheet. It costs the teacher more time and adds little.
4. Dropping *Bästa mottagare*.
5. Three grammar complaints that are not in our files (second word, perfect, question forms), and the misread review dates.
6. The 36 frames as the first layer without the official-sample lines. They are good frames, and the audit does not claim they are enough; the sample-task lines must be learned first, not later.

**The same two tests on this core.**
- **Official samples.** Each of the seven has its lines: the person talk (Describe, a person), the seller call (Describe, something you sell), the repair call (phone frame, Fix 1–4), the late voicemail (Social 4–5), the event e-mail (letter frame, Social 6–7), the letter with three reasons (Opinion) and the bill complaint (letter frame, Fix 6–7, Opinion 3).
- **Largest book groups, at frame level.** Opinion (52) and advice (13): Opinion. Request (40): Ask with the phone frame. Complaint (33), including the 18 polite objections: Fix. Describe (25), including the 21 habit, place and thing tasks: Describe. React, refuse, apologise, invite (34): Social with the 10 reactions. Tell and report (26): Story, with *Kan ni [kontakta mig]?* from Fix.
- It is about 20 frames larger than the audit's list. That is the price of covering the official samples and the habit talks.

**What is lost, honestly.** One line per rare situation (resigning, giving things away) is no longer memorised. Those are 6 of 225 book tasks. The learner looks them up in T20 when the book task comes.

**Daily load rules** (these override any day entry that breaks them):
1. At most 6 new frames and 8 new cards a day.
2. One grammar point a day, stated in one sentence.
3. Review stops at 8 minutes, finished or not.
4. Hear each new line before saying it.
5. One speaking task and one typed task. A book task replaces the day's own prompt.
6. A day is 60 minutes. If a block is added, another is removed.
7. A day is done when a changed version of the task works, not when a list is recited fast.
8. If the "done when" fails, repeat the day tomorrow with new details.
9. If a day entry holds more than rule 1 allows, split it into day a and day b. Day b uses the same tasks with new details. The calendar slips, and that is intended.

**The honest cost.** Rule 9 stretches the first phase. Days 1–14 hold 12 chunks or 15 verbs on several days, so Phase 1 becomes roughly 18–21 study days. The 84 days become a count of units, not a date.

**How T1–T20 and the 84 days become a reference library.**
- **P section 2** becomes the bank to look things up in. Core lines get a star. T-numbers stay as aliases, so the book map still works.
- **The 84 day entries** become practice units grouped by engine. The calendar (P 1134–1219) is a default order. The learner moves on by the load rules, not by date.
- **The book** becomes the question bank: each task carries an engine tag, the learner picks one or two per day, and a set is held back for mocks.

## 5. Simplified Day 1 and Day 2

### Day 1: say who you are

**Goal:** understand four questions and answer each in one full sentence.

**New frames (5):**
1. *Jag heter [Alex].* (My name is …)
2. *Jag kommer från [Indien].* (I come from …)
3. *Jag bor i [Esbo].* (I live in …)
4. *Jag talar [engelska, hindi och marathi].* (I speak …)
5. *Jag lär mig svenska.* (I am learning Swedish.)

**Questions to understand (4):** *Vad heter du?* (What is your name?) *Var kommer du ifrån?* (Where are you from?) *Var bor du?* (Where do you live?) *Vilka språk talar du?* (Which languages do you speak?)

**Instruction words to recognise (2):** *berätta* (tell), *skriv* (write).

**The one grammar fact:** the verb has the same form for every person: *jag bor, du bor*.

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | **Listen.** Hear each frame three times in a Swedish voice, with the English in view. Repeat after the voice. |
| 10–20 | **Recall and swap.** Cover the Swedish. Say each frame from the English. Then swap the detail three times (another name, country, town, language). |
| 20–30 | **Questions.** Hear the four questions in mixed order. Answer each in a full sentence within 10 seconds. |
| 30–40 | **Speaking task.** Card: *Berätta om dig själv.* (Tell about yourself.) Underline the instruction word. Record five sentences, listen once, redo once. |
| 40–50 | **Typing.** Set up å, ä and ö. Type the five sentences from memory, compare with the model, fix, and type them once more. |
| 50–55 | **Change check.** Answer the four questions again as another person: Sara, from Sweden, lives in Åbo, speaks Swedish and Finnish. |
| 55–60 | **Cards:** 5 frames and 3 questions (8 cards). Start the error log. |

**Model:** *Hej! Jag heter Alex. Jag kommer från Indien. Jag bor i Esbo. Jag talar engelska, hindi och marathi. Jag lär mig svenska.*

**Done when:** you answer 4 of 4 questions without reading, and the typed text has no more than 2 spelling slips. If not, repeat Day 1 tomorrow with new details.

**Moved out of Day 1, and where each item lands:**
- *Jag jobbar som …* and *Jag har bott …*: Day 2.
- Numbers 0–12: Day 2. Numbers 13–20: Day 3.
- G1 (verb second) with *Nu bor jag …*, *På fritiden …* and *I dag jobbar jag hemma*: the start of Day 3, which already drills time-first sentences (C 76–84). By rule 9, Day 3 then splits into 3a (G1) and 3b (*inte*).
- The two phone lines: Day 4, which already teaches the phone frame (C 115).
- The *eftersom* rung of the ladder: Day 8, as C 94 already says.
- The 60-second, 8-sentence talk: it stays as the Day 14 exit target (P 951). By then the learner has all 8 facts.
- The book warm-up `p8-U`: optional reading.

### Day 2: say more, and ask back

**Goal:** add work and time in Finland, and ask two questions yourself.

**New frames (5):**
1. *Jag jobbar som [ingenjör].* (I work as …)
2. *Jag har bott i Finland i [fem] år.* (I have lived in Finland for … years. Learn it as one piece.)
3. *Vad heter du?* (now you ask it)
4. *Var bor du?* (now you ask it)
5. *Och du?* (And you?)

**Questions to understand (2 more):** *Vad jobbar du med?* (What do you do for work?) *Hur länge har du bott i Finland?* (How long have you lived in Finland?)

**Numbers 0–12:** *noll, ett, två, tre, fyra, fem, sex, sju, åtta, nio, tio, elva, tolv.*

**Instruction words to recognise (2):** *fråga* (ask), *svara* (answer).

**The one grammar fact:** in a question, the verb comes straight after the question word: *Var bor du?*, not *Var du bor?* (C 68).

| Minutes | What you do |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | **Review** the 8 cards from Day 1, aloud. |
| 8–18 | **Listen and repeat** the new frames and the numbers 0–12. |
| 18–28 | **Turn answers into questions.** *Jag bor i Esbo.* → *Var bor du?* *Jag heter Alex.* → *Vad heter du?* Then answer all six questions in mixed order. |
| 28–40 | **Speaking task.** A helper or a recorded voice asks: *Vad heter du? Var bor du? Vad jobbar du med? Hur länge har du bott i Finland?* Answer each in one sentence. After two of them, add *Och du?* Record, listen once, redo once. |
| 40–50 | **Typing.** Card: *Skriv till en ny kurskamrat. Berätta om dig själv och ställ två frågor.* (Write to a new course-mate. Tell about yourself and ask two questions.) Underline what the card asks for. |
| 50–55 | **Number check.** Hear 8 numbers from 0 to 12 and write them as digits. |
| 55–60 | **Cards (8 at most):** 2 frames, 3 question cards, and the numbers you missed. |

**Model:** *Hej! Jag heter Alex och jag kommer från Indien. Jag bor i Esbo. Jag har bott i Finland i fem år. Jag jobbar som ingenjör. Jag lär mig svenska. Vad heter du? Var bor du? Hälsningar, Alex*

**Done when:** you answer 6 of 6 questions in full sentences, both of your own questions have the verb before *du*, and the message has a greeting, five facts, two questions and a closing.

**Moved out of Day 2, and where each item lands:**
- Numbers 20–100 and the confusable pairs: Day 4 (with the clock times) and Day 11 (prices and phone numbers), which already use them.
- The other question words: two a day on Days 3–6, to recognise. *Varför …?* on Day 8, with *eftersom*.

## 7. Limits of this adjudication

- **Coverage.** I read P and T in full and about 25 of the 84 day entries in C in full (Days 1–9, 20–22, 24, 30, 31, 37, 45, 46, 48, 52, 65, 73, 79, 82 and parts of others). The rest of C was covered by scripts: word-order searches, gender lists and book-task counts.
- **Swedish.** No native speaker has checked anything here, including my own suggestions. The checks found no outright grammar error, which is not the same as natural Swedish.
- **Official facts.** They came from a delegated web lookup. Some quotes may be the fetch tool's summary of the page, not the page's exact words. Check the dates and fees again on the registration service before paying.
- **The CSV.** The check was mechanical: line, label and day. The fit of each new tag rests on our own short descriptions of the book tasks, not on the book.
- **Minute totals.** They use our own per-task estimates (T 299). Nobody has timed a real session.
- **The new core.** It has not been tried on a learner.

## 8. Three decisions for the learner

1. **Core and reference.** Do you accept that you memorise only the core in section 4 (6 engines, about 60 frames, 12 reason pairs, 10 reactions), and that T1–T20 and the 84 days become a library you look things up in?
2. **Where fresh mock prompts come from.** The printed prompts are now seen. Choose one source for the mocks that count: your teacher writes them, an AI writes them on the day, or about 40 book tasks are held back and never used on normal days.
3. **Where the core lives.** Build the core into the app, so every line has a Swedish voice and a pronunciation score, or keep it in these documents and get audio from the teacher and the book's QR tracks.

None of these needs the exam date or the subtest pair.
