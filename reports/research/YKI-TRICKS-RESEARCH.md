# Swedish YKI level 3: exam tricks, researched independently

*Written 15 November 2026. This does not rely on the ChatGPT chat. It tests that chat's "tricks" claims against four independent streams: official sample pages (read directly), a tally of the repo's own YKI sources, a web search, and an audit of what the app already teaches. Evidence labels: **official**, **secondary**, **anecdote**, **inference** (my reasoning), **not found**. Names, dates and fees live in `FINAL-YKI-REPORT.md`; this file does not repeat them.*

**All Swedish in this file's sources is unreviewed by a native speaker.** One example: the ChatGPT complaint template says *Problemet är att telefonen fungerar inte*. In a subordinate clause *inte* goes before the verb: *att telefonen inte fungerar*.

---

## 1. Bottom line

1. **There is no shortcut that replaces Swedish, and nobody official endorses one.** OPH's only prep advice found is: "Prepare for the test and also get to know the types of questions used in the test." (official, undated)
2. **Templates work only on production (speaking and writing); strategy helps all four subtests.** *(Corrected 16 November 2026, second review: an earlier version said tricks work only on production.)* Listening and reading cannot be templated, and no strategy replaces Swedish comprehension. But they do have strategy: read the question first and search for the information it asks for; expect synonyms rather than the question's own words; listen and look for negatives and changes (*inte, aldrig, men, i stället, inställt*); catch numbers, dates and prices; and notice whether someone accepts, refuses, cancels or changes a plan. Answering every item is official advice (reading sample: no penalty for wrong answers). The rest is **inference** from general test-taking practice, not YKI-specific evidence. Strategy helps you use the Swedish you have; it does not add Swedish you lack.
3. **Task types are predictable inside the material we have.** In the Gimara book, 86% of Dialoger/Reagera speaking tasks fall into five functions. This is one author's selection, not an official task bank, so it shows predictability of the practice material, not of the real exam.
4. **About 14% of speaking tasks and 8 of 21 writing tasks fall outside the top-5 templates.** "A few templates" misses them (section 4).
5. **Whole-answer memorisation has no evidence either way for YKI.** One self-reported test-taker memorised about 25 texts and passed. Other exams (OPIc/ACTFL) train raters to flag rehearsed speech. Use slot templates, not whole answers.

## 2. What the official sample pages show (read directly, 15 Nov 2026)

- **Listening sample** (ykitesti2.solki.jyu.fi): three audio files and no question text. The only text is "three task types: multiple choice, true/false, open questions". Item counts and play counts are **not found**.
- **Reading sample:** three texts (eye-drop advert, quad-bike article with figures, police-chase report). Questions are not on the page. Instructions: answer every question; no penalty for wrong answers; one correct option; paper answer sheet; open answers handwritten. Paper-era wording.
- **Instruction page** (JYU): about 5 hours; "Always try to answer every question and task"; all subtests required for the certificate.
- **OPH digital practice tests:** four tests (reading, listening, writing, speaking) on the OPH "Före YKI-testdagen" page. The heading says they are for **Finnish** ("Övningstest för det digitala YKI-testet i finska"). A Swedish digital practice test was not found there. Whether Swedish writing is typed remains unconfirmed.

## 4. How predictable the tasks are (repo sources, hand-classified, about ±10%)

Sources: Gimara book (225 tasks), teacher's lessons 23-51, `docs/yki-phase-plan.md`.

**Speaking (Dialoger 34 + Reagera 99 = 133 tasks)**

| Function | Count |
|---|---|
| Request, ask for info, book, ask for help | 38 |
| Complaint, reproach, claim | 33 |
| React, comfort, congratulate, social | 20 |
| Describe, tell, inform | 13 |
| Advice, suggest, offer help | 11 |
| Report, notify authority | 7 |
| Opinion | 4 |
| Polite refusal | 3 |
| Apology, cancel | 3 |
| Invitation | 1 |

The top 5 functions cover 115 of 133 (about 86%). The top 2 cover 53%. 18 of 34 Dialoger tasks explicitly say phone. Dialoger have 3-8 scripted turns; Reagera are single-turn reactions. The book also has 33 Berätta (mostly personal-experience monologues) and 38 Din åsikt (agree or disagree with a statement, with bullet prompts).

**Writing (21 book tasks)**

| Function | Count |
|---|---|
| Opinion text (A or B choice) | 5 |
| Request or inquiry | 4 |
| Complaint | 3 |
| Congratulate, support, thank | 3 |
| Invitation | 2 |
| Describe a place or advise a friend | 2 |
| Polite refusal | 1 |
| Apology or explain absence | 1 |

**Moves a "few templates" strategy misses** (each is its own move): apology or cancel, polite refusal with a reason, describing a person's appearance, giving or asking directions, persuading or negotiating, reporting to an authority, public notices or adverts, thank-you or recommendation letters.

**Teacher's exam format** (Group 3 L23, unofficial): speaking is Berätta 1 min prep + 1 min talk; Dialoger 15 s to read, 10-25 s per turn; Reagera 20 s prep + 25 s speaking; Din åsikt 1 min prep + 1.5 min. Writing is one informal and one formal text of 50-80 words plus one opinion text of 100-150 words. The FINAL report says no official per-subtest times or word counts are published, so treat these as practice targets only.

**Not covered here:** the book has no listening or reading material. The Classroom listening and reading items were not read.
