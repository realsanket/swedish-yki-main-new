# Writing rubric — 7 criteria

Shared by assignments 14, 27, and 35.

## Flexibility

- **Level 4:** Can in familiar and less familiar text types adjust what he/she writes according to the purpose of the text, recipient and the situation
- **Level 3:** Manages well everyday communication situations, but cannot sufficiently express the difference between formal and informal register.
- **Level 2:** Holds simple expressions that have been learnt as phrases and can use them in written everyday communicative situations.

## Cohesion

- **Level 4:** Can use a variety of linguistic means to mark relationship between ideas and to produce coherent text, although there may be some lack of coherence in long contributions.
- **Level 3:** Can write fairly coherent text.
- **Level 2:** Can use the most frequently occurring connectors (such as ‘and’, ‘but’ and ‘because’) to link simple sentences in order to write a short text.

## Precision / range

- **Level 4:** Can convey information fairly accurately. Has a good range of vocabulary for general topics. Can avoid extensive repetition. Occasional wrong word choices and mixups do not cause misunderstanding.
- **Level 3:** Can express themself in a comprehensible, fairly accurate manner. Errors are linked to complex ideas/less familiar topics. Has sufficiently wide vocabulary related to everyday life (family, hobbies and interests, work, travel, etc.)
- **Level 2:** Can convey simple and straightforward information in familiar, routine communicative situations. Can control a narrow and concrete vocabulary that has to do with everyday needs.

## Grammatical accuracy

- **Level 4:** Controls grammatical structures relatively well. Minor flaws in structures may occur, but they not lead to misunderstanding.
- **Level 3:** The use of grammatical structures follows the requirements in familiar situations. Though in more demanding situations the use of grammatical structures may be unnatural, the message is understood.
- **Level 2:** Uses some simple structures correctly, but still systematically makes mistakes in basic structures, for example, mixes up tenses. Nevertheless, it is usually clear what he/she is trying to say.

## Communicating (narrating, reporting, describing)

- **Level 4:** Clear, detailed accounts on a variety of topics, particularly of personal interest. Can highlight essential matters, express relevant supporting examples and explain their opinion on a current issue and present pros and cons.
- **Level 3:** Can present simple descriptions of familiar topics of interest. Can fairly fluently present a simple story, by listing matters. Can explain details of unexpected event, e.g. an accident, the plot of a book/film and their reactions. Can describe dreams, hopes and aims and shortly express reasons and explanations to opinions/plans.
- **Level 2:** Can present an account, tell a story or describe something by using a simple list. Can describe everyday things and matters from his/her surroundings, such as people, places, or a work- or study-related experiences as well events and actions shortly. Can describe plans and arrangements, customs, routines and personal experiences. Can use simple descriptive language in shortly expressing something about things and about owning and comparing them.

## Creative writing

- **Level 4:** Can write clear and detailed accounts of real and imaginary events and show relationships between thoughts and follow the fixed practices related to the text style. Can write a film, book and play review.
- **Level 3:** Can write a detailed account of topic that interests him/her. Can describe feelings and reactions in a simple coherent text Can write a description of an event, such as a recently made or an imaginary trip.
- **Level 2:** With linked sentences, can write about matters related to everyday life, for example about people, places or an experience related to work or study. Can write very short, basic descriptions of events, past actions and personal experiences.

## Report / essay / opinion

- **Level 4:** Can write an essay or report, in which she/he develops an argument systematically, highlighting central issues and bringing forth supporting details, pros and cons. Can evaluate different thoughts and solutions. Can make a synthesis of information and arguments from different sources.
- **Level 3:** Can write short, simple essays on interesting topics, draw conclusions and express opinions. Can write short reports, which follow usual standard forms and which convey routine information.
- **Level 2:** Can express his/her opinion on matters related to him/her, but cannot necessarily give reasons.
